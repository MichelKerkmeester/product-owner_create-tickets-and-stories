The request is a timeline, which is one of the kinds Figma's board generator cannot draw. Per `flow-mode.md` Section 3, the nearest drawable type is a gantt chart with each event as a milestone, so this comes out as one chart with three points on a date axis.

**What the request gives**

| Milestone | Date | Distance from the point above |
|---|---|---|
| Design done | 16 November | anchors the chart |
| Beta | 7 December | 3 weeks after design done |
| Release | 21 December | 2 weeks after beta |

The spacing is the reason a board earns its place over a one-line list. Three dates in a sentence read as though they sit the same distance apart, and on an axis the reader sees a three-week gap then a two-week one, which is what they need to place reviews and comms against. If you only want the three dates recited, a single sentence does that better and I will skip the board.

What this board must not do: show build work, owners or dependencies between the three points. Nothing you sent supplies a span, an owner or a dependency, so anything between the milestones would be invented rather than read.

**Three things to settle before I put the model up**

1. Who reads it, and what do they decide from it? My answer: the Loomlist team and anyone planning around the offline mode launch, used to fix review and comms dates against the three milestones.
2. Which year? Your dates carry a day and a month and no year, and the chart cannot place a point without one. My answer: 2026, because the next 16 November falls there.
3. Committed, or still moving? My answer: committed, drawn as plain milestones. A date still in discussion would carry that status in its label so nobody plans against one that shifts.
