# FJ-025-1-3 fresh reader notes

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Checkout payment, authorised card". The subtitle states the question directly: what calls does a Roamstay checkout make, and in what order, when the card is authorised. It is a sequence diagram of the happy path only.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title and its subtitle at the top left. Second the bright pink arrow at step 3 "Authorise the card", which is the only saturated thick line on the board and pulls the eye straight to the card authorisation call. After that the four participant boxes across the top and the numbered purple step badges running down the page.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end, naming the shapes

Four participants sit in rounded rectangles across the top, left to right: GuestApp, BookingApi, PaymentProvider and PartnerHub. Each has a dashed grey lifeline running down to a smaller rounded box repeating its name at the bottom. Seven numbered steps run top to bottom, each marked by a purple circle badge:

1. GuestApp to BookingApi, solid grey request "Post the booking".
2. BookingApi to itself, a solid grey self-call loop "Hold the room".
3. BookingApi to PaymentProvider, thick pink request "Authorise the card" (the step in focus).
4. PaymentProvider back to BookingApi, dashed grey reply "Card authorised".
5. BookingApi to itself, solid grey self-call loop "Confirm the hold".
6. BookingApi to PartnerHub, thin blue async message "Tell the hub".
7. BookingApi back to GuestApp, dashed grey reply "201 Created".

The run starts with the guest app posting a booking and ends with the guest app receiving 201 Created.

## 4 Relationships, messages and timing

This is a sequence board, so there are no decision diamonds. The order is strictly top to bottom and the step numbers match that order. BookingApi is the hub of every exchange: it receives the booking, talks to itself twice, calls the payment provider and waits for its reply, then notifies PartnerHub and answers the guest app. The payment call is synchronous (request then dashed reply). The PartnerHub call is asynchronous with no reply drawn, so BookingApi does not wait for the hub before returning 201 Created. The only branch in the real flow, a declined card, is deliberately left off. The footer says the declined-card path (release the hold and 402) is kept for a second board.

## 5 What the key says and whether every mark is in it

The key, in a light grey panel below the diagram, lists:

- Participant: a white rounded rectangle with grey outline.
- Request: a solid grey line with a small filled arrowhead.
- Reply: a dashed grey line with a filled arrowhead.
- Async message: a thin blue line with an open arrowhead.
- Step number: a purple filled circle with a white number.
- In focus: a thick pink line with a large filled arrowhead.

Nearly every mark is covered. Marks not explicitly keyed: the dashed grey vertical lifelines, the smaller repeated participant boxes at the bottom and the self-call loop shape at steps 2 and 5 (it uses the request line style, so it reads as a request to itself). These are standard sequence diagram conventions and did not block reading.

## 6 Anything unreadable, unfollowable or ambiguous

All text is legible at 100 percent. Minor points:

- The pink "In focus" line at step 3 replaces the request style, so the key leaves it to the reader to infer that step 3 is also a synchronous request. The dashed reply at step 4 confirms it.
- Step badges sit on different sides of their arrows (left of the line for steps 1 to 3 and 5 to 6, right of the lifeline for steps 4 and 7). It works because they sit at the sending end, but the rule is not stated.
- Whether "Tell the hub" can fail, and whether its failure matters to the booking, is not shown.
- "201 Created" and "402" are HTTP codes that a non-technical reader might not know.
- The footer names the source only as "the payments brief" with no link.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, because the title, ordered numbered steps, complete key and footer note on the excluded declined path make the authorised checkout sequence clear on its own, though they would need the second board for the decline case.
