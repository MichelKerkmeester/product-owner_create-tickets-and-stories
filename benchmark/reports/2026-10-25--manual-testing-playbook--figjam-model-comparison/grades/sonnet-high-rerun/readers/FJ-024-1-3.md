# Reader notes: FJ-024-1-3

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Loomlist block sync". The subtitle reads "The services that carry a block change from a Loomlist client to a woken iOS device, for engineers on sync". The board answers this question: which services does a block change pass through between a client editing it and an iOS device being woken by a push?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the title and then the thick pink outline around sync-service in the middle of the board. It is the strongest mark on the page. Second I see the row of tinted group frames running left to right (Clients, Edge, Services, Events, Stores, Outside) and the key panel along the bottom.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

This is an architecture or service map, read left to right.

- Clients frame on the left holds three teal rounded rectangles: Desktop client, Web client and iOS client.
- Each client connects to the API gateway (a blue rounded rectangle in the Edge frame) by a solid line labelled "Live socket" with arrowheads at both ends.
- API gateway goes to sync-service (white rounded rectangle with a pink focus outline in the Services frame) by a solid arrow labelled "Routes block changes".
- sync-service goes up and across the top of the board by a solid arrow labelled "Writes blocks" into Blocks database (purple cylinder in the Stores frame).
- sync-service also goes right by a dashed arrow labelled "Publishes changes" into Change stream (orange horizontal cylinder in the Events frame).
- Change stream goes down and back left by a dashed arrow labelled "Consumes changes" into Push worker (white rounded rectangle, lower in the Services frame).
- Push worker goes right by a long dashed arrow labelled "Apple Push: Wakes iOS devices" into Apple Push (grey rounded rectangle with a dashed outline in the Outside frame).

The path ends at Apple Push.

## 4 Decisions, branches, relationships, messages or timing

There are no decision shapes. The one fork is at sync-service, which sends each change two ways: a solid (direct) write to Blocks database and a dashed (async) publish to Change stream. Timing is shown by line style. The client to gateway sockets are two way and live. Gateway to sync-service and sync-service to the database are direct calls. Publishing to the stream, consuming from it and calling Apple Push are async or outside calls. The board does not show who listens to the database or whether anything reads back from it.

## 5 What the key says

The key lists:

- Teal rounded rectangle: App or client
- Blue rounded rectangle: Edge or gateway
- White rounded rectangle: Service
- Orange horizontal cylinder: Queue or stream
- Purple vertical cylinder: Data store
- Grey rounded rectangle with dashed outline: Outside system
- Solid line with one arrowhead: Leads to
- Dashed line with one arrowhead: Async or outside call
- Solid line with arrowheads at both ends: Both ways
- Thick pink outline: "In focus: Writes every block change"

Every node, line style and the pink outline on the board appears in the key. The only marks not keyed are the tinted group frames and their fill colours (pale blue for Clients and Stores, peach for Edge, mint for Services, pale yellow for Events, pale lilac for Outside). Their names are written on them so I could read them as groupings, but the key does not say whether the frame colours mean anything.

## 6 What I could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous

- The subtitle promises the change reaches "a woken iOS device", but no line goes from Apple Push back to the iOS client or to any device. The last hop is implied by the edge label only (bottom right, between Push worker and Apple Push).
- The "Writes blocks" line leaves the top of sync-service and runs over the top of the Events frame before dropping into Blocks database. It reads fine but at a glance it looks as if it might belong to Events.
- The Live socket lines are both ways, so the board does not say which client starts a change or whether other desktop and web clients get the change back over their socket. Only the iOS push path is drawn.
- The footer reads "Source: the request for this board. Left out: file storage, no stated connection and over the node budget." I could read it but the phrasing is terse. I take it to mean file storage was left out because no connection was stated and the board was at its node limit.
- The pink focus note "Writes every block change" names only the database write, while sync-service also publishes. That is consistent but a little narrower than what the arrows show.
- All text was legible at 100 percent. Edge labels are small but readable.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone?

Yes for understanding, because the path from client through gateway, sync-service, stream and push worker to Apple Push is complete and fully keyed, though they would have to infer the final wake of the iOS device since no line draws it.
