# Reader notes: Block sync board

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Block sync". The subtitle reads "The parts a block change passes through in Loomlist sync, for engineers and the product owner". The board answers which systems a single block change travels through when a client syncs it, from the app to storage and on to push notifications.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title, then the Sync Service box, which has a thick pink outline and stands out more than any other shape. Second my eye goes to the three teal client pills on the left and follows the arrows rightward to the API Gateway.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

This is an architecture or component map read left to right in six labelled group frames: Clients, Edge, Services, Events, Stores and External.

- Clients holds three teal rounded pills: Desktop App, Web App and iOS App. Each sends a solid arrow labelled "Syncs blocks" to the API Gateway.
- Edge holds one blue rounded pill, API Gateway. A solid arrow labelled "Routes sync" goes to Sync Service.
- Services holds Sync Service (white pill with the pink focus outline) and Push Worker (plain white pill).
- From Sync Service a solid line labelled "Writes Blocks" rises above the Events frame, runs right and drops into Blocks DB, a purple cylinder in the Stores frame.
- From Sync Service a dashed arrow labelled "Publishes" goes to Change Stream, an orange horizontal cylinder in the Events frame.
- From Change Stream a dashed line labelled "Consumes" runs down and left into Push Worker.
- From Push Worker a long dashed arrow labelled "Apple Push: Sends notifications" goes to Apple Push, a grey dashed pill in the External frame.

The end points are Blocks DB (stored change) and Apple Push (notification sent).

## 4 Branches, relationships and timing

There are no decision diamonds. The one fork is at Sync Service, which does two things: a solid (direct) write to Blocks DB and a dashed (async) publish to Change Stream. The async branch continues through Push Worker to Apple Push. The three client arrows converge on the API Gateway. Solid lines read as direct calls and dashed lines as asynchronous or outside calls, so the database write is synchronous and everything after the publish happens later. No order is stated between the write and the publish.

## 5 What the key says

- Teal filled rounded pill: App or client
- Blue filled pill with a blue outline: Edge or gateway
- White pill with grey outline: Service
- Orange cylinder on its side: Queue or stream
- Purple upright cylinder: Data store
- Grey fill with dashed outline: Outside system
- Solid arrow: Leads to
- Dashed arrow: Async or outside call
- Thick pink outline: In focus

Every node and line style on the board appears in the key. The pastel background colours of the six group frames (light blue, peach, mint, pale yellow, pale blue and lilac) are not explained in the key, though their titles make their role clear enough. A footnote reads "Source: the request, connections assumed, not yet confirmed. Left out: file storage, off the sync path".

## 6 Unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

- All text is readable at both zoom levels.
- The "Writes Blocks" line leaves the top of Sync Service, passes over the top of the Events frame and enters Blocks DB from the left. It is easy to follow but long, and "Blocks" is capitalised unlike every other edge label.
- The label "Apple Push: Sends notifications" repeats the target name, which the other labels do not do.
- The board never shows how the change reaches the other clients. Only iOS has a delivery route (through Apple Push). Desktop App and Web App receive nothing, and nothing reads from Blocks DB, so the return half of sync is missing or out of scope without saying so.
- Why Sync Service is "in focus" is not explained anywhere on the board.
- The footnote says the connections are assumed and unconfirmed, so every arrow carries that caveat.
- The group frame colours have no key entry, as noted above.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

No, because the footnote says the connections are unconfirmed and the board does not show how desktop and web clients receive a change or why Sync Service is in focus, so a product manager would need to check those points before acting.
