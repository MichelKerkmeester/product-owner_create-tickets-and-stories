# Reader notes: Start a return 16

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Start a return 16". The subtitle says the board shows how a Fernhouse customer starts a return in the app, from the order list to a confirmed return. The question it answers is: what steps and choices does a customer go through in the app to get a return confirmed?

## 2 What you see first and what second

First I see the large bold title and then the bright pink outlined green box "Return confirmed" at the far right, which pulls the eye harder than anything else on the board. Second I see the three tinted section panels in a row ("Find the order", "Request the return" and "Done") with the two yellow diamonds inside the middle panel.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

The flow reads left to right across three sections.

In "Find the order" it starts at a blue rounded box "Order list", which leads by an arrow to a white rounded box "Order detail".

From there an arrow crosses into "Request the return" and reaches a yellow diamond "Within 30 days?". The Yes exit, labelled "Yes, Start a return", leads to a white rounded box "Return reason". That leads to a second yellow diamond "Drop-off or pickup?".

Both exits of that diamond lead into the "Done" section and end at the green rounded box "Return confirmed", which carries a thick pink outline.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

"Within 30 days?" has two exits. "Yes, Start a return" goes right to "Return reason". "No" drops down from the bottom of the diamond and goes right to a peach box with a dashed orange outline, "Request an exception". Nothing leaves that box, so the No path ends there.

"Drop-off or pickup?" has two exits. "Courier pickup" goes straight right into "Return confirmed". "Drop-off point" leaves from the top of the diamond, runs along above the panels and comes down into the top of "Return confirmed". Both choices end at the same goal.

## 5 What the key says and whether every mark is in the key

The key at the bottom lists seven entries. A blue rounded box means "Where it starts". A white rounded box with a grey outline means "Screen or step". A yellow diamond means "Decision". A green rounded box with a green outline means "Goal reached". A peach box with a dashed orange outline means "Leaves the flow". A thin solid arrow means "Leads to". A green box with a thick pink outline means "In focus".

Almost every mark is covered. The marks not in the key are the three tinted section panels (peach, mint and cream backgrounds) which are explained only by their own headings and the text labels on the arrows which explain themselves. "Return confirmed" uses the "In focus" style rather than the plain "Goal reached" style, so the green outline swatch from the key does not appear anywhere on the board itself. I read the box as both the goal and the focus because of its green fill, but the key does not say a box can be both.

## 6 Anything I could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous

The "16" in the title is not explained anywhere. It could be a version, a ticket number or a scenario number.

The board does not say why "Return confirmed" is in focus or what the reader is meant to look at there.

The No path ends at "Request an exception" with nothing after it. I cannot tell whether an approved exception rejoins the flow at "Return reason" or what the customer sees next.

The arrow from "Return reason" into "Drop-off or pickup?" has no label, which is fine, but I cannot tell whether "Return reason" is a screen where the customer also picks the method or two separate screens.

The "Drop-off point" line runs above the "Request the return" panel and crosses the panel edge near the top right. It is easy to follow but sits outside the panels, so it is slightly loose.

The footnote says the sources were the Fernhouse app, the returns doc and a later team update and that free pickup over EUR 100 was left out as a proposal under review. It does not say whether courier pickup costs money today, which matters for the "Courier pickup" branch.

The label "Yes, Start a return" uses a capital S mid sentence, which reads like a button name but is not marked as one.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Mostly yes for the happy path, since every step and both decisions are clear and both return methods reach the confirmed goal, but no for the full flow because the exception path stops dead and the "16" in the title and the focus highlight are unexplained.
