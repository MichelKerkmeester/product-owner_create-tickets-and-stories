# FJ-027-1-3 fresh reader notes

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Stays model". The subtitle reads "The six tables behind a stay, with the guest tables apart from the partner tables". The board answers the question: which tables make up a stay booking, which keys join them and how many rows of one table can sit against a row of another.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title and then the Booking box, which is the only box with a thick pink outline and sits top right inside the light blue "Guest side" panel. Second I see the two coloured panels, light blue "Guest side" at the top and light orange "Partner side" at the bottom left, with the grey Key panel along the bottom.

## 3 The main structure from start to end, naming the shapes

This is an entity relationship diagram, not a flow, so there is no start or end. It holds six white rectangles with grey outlines, one per table, each listing its keys.

- Guest side panel: Guest (PK id), Booking (PK id, FK guestId, FK roomId, FK ratePlanId) and Payment (PK id, FK bookingId).
- Partner side panel: Property (PK id), Room (PK id, FK propertyId) and Rate Plan (PK id, FK propertyId).

Booking is the hub. Three tables point into it (Guest, Room and Rate Plan) and one hangs off it (Payment). Property is the second hub on the partner side and owns Room and Rate Plan.

## 4 The relationships it shows

Every line is a solid grey connector with a verb label and a cardinality in brackets, plus crow's foot end marks.

- Guest "makes (1 to 0..n)" Booking. Double bar at Guest, circle plus crow's foot at Booking. One guest has zero or many bookings. The line runs along the top of the blue panel.
- Booking "holds (1 to 0..n)" Payment. Double bar at Booking, circle plus crow's foot at Payment. One booking has zero or many payments.
- Room "is reserved by (1 to 0..n)" Booking. Double bar at Room, circle plus crow's foot entering the left side of Booking. One room has zero or many bookings. This line crosses out of the orange panel and up into the blue one.
- Rate Plan "prices (1 to 0..n)" Booking. Double bar at Rate Plan, circle plus crow's foot entering the bottom of Booking. One rate plan prices zero or many bookings.
- Property "offers (1 to 1..n)" Room. Double bar at Property, bar plus crow's foot at Room. Every property has at least one room.
- Property "publishes (1 to 1..n)" Rate Plan. Same marks. Every property has at least one rate plan.

The foreign keys listed in each box match these lines: guestId, roomId and ratePlanId on Booking, bookingId on Payment and propertyId on Room and Rate Plan. No messages or timing are shown.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in it

The key has three entries.

- White rectangle with grey outline: Entity.
- Plain solid grey line: Identifying.
- Rectangle with thick pink outline: In focus.

Marks on the board that the key does not explain:

- The crow's foot end marks (double bar, circle with crow's foot, bar with crow's foot). The bracketed text on each line spells out the cardinality, so they can be read without the key, but the key itself says nothing about them.
- The light blue and light orange panel fills. Their headings ("Guest side" and "Partner side") name them, so they are labelled but not keyed.
- "PK", "FK" and the word "key" after each column name are not explained.

The key names a solid line "Identifying" but there is no other line style to contrast it with, and every line on the board is solid.

## 6 What I could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous

- Line style meaning, in the Key panel and on every connector: calling every line "Identifying" reads oddly. In an identifying relationship the parent key is part of the child's primary key, but each child here has its own "PK id" and the foreign keys are plain FK columns. I could not tell whether the author means identifying in the strict sense or just "a relationship".
- Why Booking is "In focus", top right: the pink outline marks it but nothing on the board says what the focus is for (a change, a question, a review).
- Label placement below Booking: the "is reserved by (1 to 0..n)" label sits in the gap between the Room line and the Rate Plan line where both run vertically close together. It sits on the left line so it belongs to Room, but it takes a second look.
- The word "key" after every column (for example "PK id key") is unclear. It may be a column type placeholder or a leftover.
- The footer reads "Source: your request, keys implied by the joins. Left out: the columns support asks about, not supplied." It tells me the non key columns are missing on purpose, so the tables show keys only and no data columns.
- Everything else was legible at 100 percent and the overview and full images match.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for understanding the shape of the model, since the six tables, their keys and every cardinality are written out in plain text, but no for acting on it as a schema, because the board leaves out every non key column and does not say why Booking is in focus.
