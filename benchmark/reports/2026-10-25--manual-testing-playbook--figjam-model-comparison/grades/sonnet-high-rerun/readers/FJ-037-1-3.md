# Reader notes: FJ-037-1-3

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Self-serve return (proposed)". The subtitle reads "How a Fernhouse customer would start a return from an order, from the delivered order to a received request". The board answers this question: when a customer has a delivered order, how would they start a return on their own and what happens if they are too late?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title and its two-line subtitle at top left. Second the bright pink outlined green box "Request received" at the far right of the flow row, because it is the only shape with a thick pink outline and it pulls the eye. After that the yellow diamond in the middle of the row.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

The flow runs left to right in one row. It starts at a light blue rounded box "Delivered order". An arrow leads to a yellow diamond "Within 30 days of delivery?". From the diamond the "Yes" arrow leads right to a white rounded box with a grey outline "Start a return". An arrow from there leads to a green rounded box "Request received", which carries the thick pink outline. That is the end of the main path.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

There is one decision, the diamond "Within 30 days of delivery?". It has two exits. "Yes" leaves the right point of the diamond and goes to "Start a return", which then leads to "Request received". "No" leaves the bottom point of the diamond, bends right and goes to a peach rounded box with a dashed orange outline "Told the window ended". That box has no arrow out, so the No branch ends there.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in the key

The key sits in a grey panel below the flow. It says:

- Light blue rounded box with blue outline: Where it starts.
- White rounded box with grey outline: Screen or step.
- Yellow diamond: Decision.
- Green rounded box with green outline: Goal reached.
- Peach rounded box with dashed orange outline: Leaves the flow.
- Thin grey line with an arrowhead: Leads to.
- Green box with thick pink outline: In focus.

Every shape and line on the board matches an entry in the key. The "Yes" and "No" words on the arrows are not in the key, but they read plainly as the answers to the diamond. One small mismatch: "Request received" shows a pink outline rather than the green outline the key gives for Goal reached, so a reader has to infer that the pink outline sits on top of a Goal reached box. The green fill makes that inference easy.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

All text is readable at both sizes. The flow is easy to follow. Points I found ambiguous:

- "Start a return" in the middle of the Yes path is one box. It does not say what the customer does on that screen, such as picking items or giving a reason, or how they submit.
- "Request received" does not say who receives it or what the customer sees or gets as confirmation.
- "Told the window ended" at the end of the No branch gives no next step for the customer, such as contacting support. The footnote says CS handling is left out, which explains the gap but leaves the customer at a dead end on the board.
- The diamond does not say how the 30 days are counted or whether day 30 itself counts as within.
- The board does not say where the customer starts from, such as an order page or an email link. The footnote says "No screens supplied".
- It is unclear why "Request received" is marked In focus when the board shows no other area of attention.

The footnote at the bottom reads "Sources: the Story and the Fernhouse company context. No screens supplied. Left out: CS handling, the refund and the warehouse check."

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for the shape of the flow, since the start, the single 30-day rule, both outcomes and the goal are clear and keyed, but they would still need to ask what the "Start a return" screen contains and what a customer past the window should do next before building it.
