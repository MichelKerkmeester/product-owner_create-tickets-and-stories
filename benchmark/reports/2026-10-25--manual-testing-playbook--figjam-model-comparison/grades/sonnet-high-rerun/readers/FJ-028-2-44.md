# Reader notes for FJ-028-2-44

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Offline mode launch plan". The subtitle states the question: which offline mode work runs when, from 2 November 2026 to a beta for 5 percent of workspaces, and what holds the release. It is a timeline (Gantt style) board with weekly columns from 2 Nov to 21 Dec.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title and its two line subtitle at the top left. Second the strong coloured bars, in particular the salmon bars with orange outlines and the one bar with a thick magenta outline in the Build lane around 30 Nov to 7 Dec. That magenta bar pulls the eye more than anything else on the timeline.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

The board has a date axis across the top (2 Nov, 9 Nov, 16 Nov, 23 Nov, 30 Nov, 7 Dec, 14 Dec, 21 Dec) with light vertical gridlines. Below it sit three stacked lanes, each a tinted rounded panel with a bold lane name and task names on the left.

- Design lane (light blue): Conflict screen design, a salmon bar with a heavy orange outline from 2 Nov to 16 Nov, labelled "2 weeks, critical".
- Build lane (light orange): Sync engine, a white bar with a grey outline from 2 Nov to 23 Nov, labelled "3 weeks". Conflict screen build, a salmon bar with a heavy orange outline from 16 Nov to 30 Nov, labelled "2 weeks, critical". Desktop and iOS integration, a salmon bar with a thick magenta outline from 30 Nov to 7 Dec, labelled "1 week, critical".
- Rollout lane (light teal): Beta for 5 percent, a white bar with a grey outline from 7 Dec to 21 Dec, labelled "2 weeks". Release, a small yellow diamond at 21 Dec.

The main path reads: Conflict screen design, then Conflict screen build, then Desktop and iOS integration, then Beta for 5 percent, then the Release diamond. Sync engine runs in parallel from the start and also feeds the integration.

Under the lanes is a Key panel and below it a source line: "Source: the offline mode launch plan. Left out: Android next quarter, not yet approved."

## 4 Relationships and timing

This is not a flow with decisions, so there are no branches or exits. The thin grey arrows show dependencies:

- From the end of Conflict screen design (16 Nov) an arrow drops straight down to the start of Conflict screen build. On its way it crosses over the Sync engine bar.
- From the end of Sync engine (23 Nov) an arrow runs right, bends down and ends at the start of Desktop and iOS integration (30 Nov). This leaves about one week of gap between Sync engine finishing and integration starting, which reads as slack.
- From the end of Conflict screen build (30 Nov) a short hooked arrow drops into the start of Desktop and iOS integration. Its arrowhead lands on the same spot as the Sync engine arrowhead, so the integration waits on both.
- From the end of Desktop and iOS integration (7 Dec) an arrow drops down to the start of Beta for 5 percent.
- From the end of Beta for 5 percent (21 Dec) a short arrow points down to the Release diamond.

Timing: 2 weeks design, 2 weeks build of the conflict screen, 1 week integration, 2 weeks beta, so release lands on 21 Dec. The chain that "holds the release" appears to be the critical bars: conflict screen design, conflict screen build and integration.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in it

The key lists five items:

- White rounded bar with grey outline: Planned.
- Salmon rounded bar with heavy orange outline: Critical, heavy outline.
- Yellow diamond: Milestone.
- Thin grey arrow: Leads to.
- Salmon rounded bar with thick magenta outline: In focus.

Every bar, the diamond and the arrows match a key entry. Marks not in the key: the three lane tint colours (blue for Design, orange for Build, teal for Rollout) and the vertical gridlines. Those read as plain grouping and date guides, so their absence is minor.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

- The Desktop and iOS integration bar (Build lane, 30 Nov to 7 Dec) is labelled "critical" but wears the magenta "In focus" outline, not the orange critical outline. The key does not say whether In focus also implies critical or replaces it, and it does not say why this task is in focus.
- Beta for 5 percent (Rollout lane, 7 Dec to 21 Dec) is drawn as Planned, not critical, even though it sits directly between the critical integration and the Release diamond. If the beta slips the release must slip too, so it is unclear why it is not marked critical, and that weakens the answer to "what holds the release".
- The two arrows into the start of Desktop and iOS integration (around 30 Nov) end on top of each other, so at overview zoom it takes a close look to see that two dependencies arrive there.
- The arrow from Conflict screen design down to Conflict screen build (16 Nov) passes straight through the Sync engine bar, which can briefly look like it starts or ends on Sync engine.
- The Release diamond has no date label of its own, it is read off the 21 Dec column. The subtitle says the plan runs "to a beta", but the board actually continues to a Release after the beta, which is a small mismatch in wording.
- All text was readable at 100 percent zoom.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, mostly: the dates, durations, dependencies and the critical chain to a 21 Dec release are clear enough to plan from, though a product manager would likely want to confirm what "In focus" means for the integration task and whether the beta counts as critical.
