# Reader notes: Damaged-item refund

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Damaged-item refund". The subtitle says it shows how a damaged-item claim moves between the customer, CS and the warehouse, from the report to the released refund. The question it answers is: who does what, and in which order, to get from a customer reporting a damaged item to a refund being released?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title and then the three wide coloured lanes stacked top to bottom (Customer in peach, CS agent in pale cyan, Warehouse in pale cream). Inside the lanes the eye goes first to the bright pink "Approve the claim" box with its thick magenta outline, because it is the strongest colour on the board. Second I notice the yellow diamond "Damage clear?" next to it and the green "Refund released" box at the bottom right, which reads as the end point.

## 3 The main path from start to end

1. "Report damage with a photo" is a blue rounded box in the Customer lane, top left. It is the start.
2. A connector runs down out of it into the CS agent lane and enters the yellow diamond "Damage clear?".
3. On "Clear" the flow runs right to the pink rounded box "Approve the claim" in the CS agent lane.
4. From there a connector goes right and then down across the lane border, labelled "To warehouse queue", into the Warehouse lane.
5. It reaches the white rounded box "Book a courier pickup".
6. Then the white rounded box "Inspect the item".
7. Then the green rounded box "Refund released", the end of the flow, at the bottom right.

## 4 Every decision and where each exit goes

There is one decision, the diamond "Damage clear?" in the CS agent lane.

- "Clear" exits right and goes to "Approve the claim".
- "Unclear" exits from the top of the diamond, runs right and up into the Customer lane, and ends at the cyan parallelogram "Send a second photo". From that parallelogram a connector runs left and down back into the left point of the "Damage clear?" diamond, so the unclear case is a loop that asks again.

There are no other branches. "Book a courier pickup" and "Inspect the item" are plain steps with a single exit each.

## 5 What the key says

The key at the bottom lists:

- Blue rounded box: Where it starts.
- White rounded box with grey outline: Step.
- Cyan parallelogram: Input or output.
- Yellow diamond: Decision.
- Green rounded box with green outline: Goal reached.
- Thin grey line with an arrowhead: Leads to.
- Pink rounded box with a thick magenta outline: In focus: Handoff to warehouse.

Every shape on the board matches a key entry. The marks that are not in the key are the three lane background colours (peach, pale cyan and pale cream). They are explained by the lane names written inside them rather than by the key, so they cause no confusion. The connector labels "Clear", "Unclear" and "To warehouse queue" are text on lines and need no key entry. The rounded corner on the connector leaving "Approve the claim" is a styling detail and is not a different line style. All connectors use the same thin solid grey line.

## 6 Anything unclear, unreadable or ambiguous

- The focus marker. The key says the pink box means "In focus: Handoff to warehouse", but the box that carries it is "Approve the claim", a CS agent step. The actual handoff is the connector labelled "To warehouse queue" that leaves that box. I read it as "the approval is the moment of handoff", but a reader could wonder whether approval itself or the queue transfer is the thing under discussion.
- The "To warehouse queue" label sits right on the gap between the CS agent and Warehouse lanes, centre of the board. It is readable but belongs to neither lane, so it is not clear who owns the queue.
- The first connector from "Report damage with a photo" enters the diamond on its lower left edge, not at a corner, while the loop back from "Send a second photo" enters at the left point. Both are readable, but the two arrows arrive close together at the left of the diamond and take a moment to separate.
- There is no rejection path. The decision only has "Clear" and "Unclear", so the board does not say what happens if the damage is clearly not covered, or if the customer never sends a second photo, or how many times the unclear loop can repeat.
- "Inspect the item" has a single exit straight to "Refund released". The board does not say what happens if the inspection does not confirm the damage.
- The footnote at the bottom left reads "Source: the support runbook. Left out: pickup skipped under €20, a pilot that is not live. Logging every decision in the claims table." The first two parts are clear. The last sentence is ambiguous: it could mean every decision is logged in a claims table as current practice, or it could be a note that logging is planned. It is also not shown anywhere on the diagram itself.
- Nothing on the board was too small or blurred to read at either zoom level.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for the happy path and the unclear-photo loop, since every step, owner and handoff is labelled and keyed, but no for the full process, because the board gives no outcome for a rejected claim, an abandoned second photo or a failed inspection, so a product manager would have to ask what happens in those cases.
