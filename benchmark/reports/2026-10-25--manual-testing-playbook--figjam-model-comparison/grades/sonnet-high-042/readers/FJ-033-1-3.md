# Fresh reader: FJ-033-1-3

## Three questions

Q1: Which outside service does Loomlist depend on?
Apple's push service. It is the grey dashed box in the group labelled Outside at the far right of the board. Sure: high.

Q2: What do the apps talk to?
All three apps (Desktop app, Web app and iOS app) send "Syncs blocks" arrows to the Entry point in the Entry group, left of centre. They do not talk to the Sync platform directly. Sure: high. The footnote says the Entry point itself is assumed, so the existence of that middle step is less certain than the arrows look.

Q3: What happens when something changes while an iPhone sleeps?
The Sync platform makes a dashed call to Apple's push service, labelled "Apple's push service: Wakes iPhones", and that service wakes the phone. Sure: medium to high. The dashed line runs below the middle row from the Sync platform to the Outside group. The last step from Apple's service to the iPhone is not drawn, and the footnote at the bottom says it was left out on purpose.

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Block sync". The subtitle says it shows how a block change from any Loomlist app is stored and wakes a sleeping iPhone, for the leadership update. So the question is: where does a change go once someone makes it, and how does a sleeping iPhone find out?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large title and subtitle at the top left. Second the Sync platform, because its thick pink outline is the strongest mark on the board. After that the eye moves left to right along the middle row of coloured groups.

## 3 The main path from start to end

It reads left to right. The Apps group holds three teal rounded boxes: Desktop app, Web app and iOS app. Each sends a solid "Syncs blocks" arrow into the Entry point, a light blue rounded box in the Entry group. The Entry point sends a solid "Passes changes on" arrow to the Sync platform, a white rounded box with a pink outline in the Platform group. The Sync platform sends a solid "Stores every change" arrow to the Change store, a purple cylinder in the Storage group. Separately, a dashed line drops from the bottom of the Sync platform and runs right to Apple's push service, a grey dashed box in the Outside group.

## 4 Branches and relationships

This is an architecture picture, not a decision flow, so there are no yes or no branches. The relationships are three apps feeding one entry point, the entry point feeding one sync platform, and the sync platform fanning out two ways. One way is the solid arrow to the Change store, which stores every change. The other is the dashed arrow to Apple's push service, which wakes iPhones.

## 5 What the key says

Teal rounded box means app or client. Light blue rounded box with a blue outline means edge or gateway. White rounded box means service. Purple cylinder means data store. Grey box with a dashed outline means outside system. A solid arrow means leads to. A dashed arrow means async or outside call. A thick pink outline means in focus: every change lands here.

Every shape and line on the board appears in the key. The coloured group backgrounds (Apps, Entry, Platform, Storage and Outside) are not in the key, but their titles explain them, so they need no entry. The Sync platform combines two key entries, a white service box and the pink focus outline, which reads clearly.

## 6 What I could not read, follow or found ambiguous

Everything is legible. Three small points. First, the dashed arrow's label "Apple's push service: Wakes iPhones" repeats the destination name, which is a little odd but not confusing. Second, the iPhone that gets woken is not on the board, so a reader has to trust the footnote that Apple's service delivers to the phone. Third, the footnote says the Entry point is assumed, which means one box on the main path is a guess rather than a known fact. It also leaves open whether the Desktop and Web apps get woken in some other way, since only iPhones are mentioned.

## 7 Could I act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, for a leadership level understanding: the path, the single focus point and the one outside dependency are all clear, though I would ask an engineer to confirm the assumed Entry point before quoting it as fact.
