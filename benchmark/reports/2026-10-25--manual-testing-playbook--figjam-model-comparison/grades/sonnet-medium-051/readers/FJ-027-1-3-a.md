The labelled lines between boxes are read next, starting with the long ones that go to Booking. The Key box and the footnote come last. In the key the swatches are Entity, Identifying and In focus.

## Three questions

Q1: How many rooms does one booking reserve? One. Booking holds a single roomId, and the line between Room and Booking is labelled "is reserved by (1 to 0..n)", which reads as one room is reserved by zero or more bookings. Fairly sure. The single roomId column is the firmer evidence, found inside Booking. The cardinality label alone is easy to read the wrong way round.

Q2: Which entity publishes rate plans? Property, through the line labelled "publishes (1 to 1..n)" between Property and Rate Plan. Rate Plan also holds a propertyId. Very sure, found at the bottom left of the board.

Q3: Can one booking hold several payments? Yes. The line "holds (1 to 1..n)" between Booking and Payment says one booking holds one or more payments, and Payment has a bookingId column. Sure, found on the right of the board.

## 1. Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Stays model". The subtitle says: the tables behind a stay and the keys that join them, split into the guest side and the partner side. The board answers which tables a stay is built from and how they join.

## 2. The main path or structure from start to end

This is an entity diagram, so there is no start or end. Booking sits at the centre of it. On the guest side Guest "makes (1 to 0..n)" Booking and Booking "holds (1 to 1..n)" Payment. Crossing into the partner side, Room "is reserved by (1 to 0..n)" Booking and Rate Plan "prices (1 to 0..n)" Booking. Inside the partner side Property "offers (1 to 1..n)" Room and Property "publishes (1 to 1..n)" Rate Plan.

Each box lists its columns. Guest has id. Booking has id plus guestId, roomId and ratePlanId. Payment has id and bookingId. Property has id. Room has id and propertyId. Rate Plan has id and propertyId.

## 3. Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

There are no decisions. The only branching is that Booking has four lines going out (to Guest, Payment, Room and Rate Plan), and Property has two (to Room and Rate Plan).

## 4. What the key says and whether every mark is in the key

- Plain white box: entity. Used on all six tables.
- Plain line: identifying. Used on every connection.
- Box with a thick magenta outline: in focus. Used on Booking.

The line ends have small crow's foot and bar marks that show how many on each side, and the labels give the counts in the form "(1 to 0..n)". Neither the line end marks nor the two zone colours are in the key. The zones are labelled in their own corners. PK and FK in the boxes are also not in the key but are plain column labels.

## 5. Anything I could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous

- The direction of each label is not marked, so "(1 to 0..n)" has to be read from the verb. "Room is reserved by Booking (1 to 0..n)" puts the 1 on the Room side and the 0..n on the Booking side. This is where I was least sure.
- The "is reserved by" line leaves from the left side of Booking near the top of the "is reserved by" label and runs a long way. It is readable but I had to follow it from Room.
- Booking holds the FK to Room and to Rate Plan, so Booking is the many side in both. That matches the 0..n side of the labels.
- The line labels read from one end, while the bookingId FK is on Payment, so "holds" is a one-to-many with the foreign key on the many side. That is consistent.
- The footnote leaves out the support-facing columns, so the tables are known to be incomplete.
- Whether a booking can exist without a Payment is unclear. The label says 1..n, which would mean at least one, but a booking may be created before the first payment.

## 6. Could I act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, for the tables, keys and join directions, because every join is labelled with a count and each FK is listed, though I would ask whether a booking can exist before its first payment.

Order: Booking > Guest > Payment > Room > Rate Plan > Property
