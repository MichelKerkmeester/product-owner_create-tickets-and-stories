## 1. Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Damaged-item refund". The subtitle says: who handles a damaged-item claim, from the customer's report to the released refund. The board answers who does what, lane by lane, once a customer reports a damaged item.

## 2. The main path or structure from start to end

Three horizontal lanes stacked top to bottom: Customer, CS agent, Warehouse operator. The path starts at "Damage reported" (blue, Customer lane) and goes down into the CS agent lane to the diamond "Damage clear in the photo?". On "Clear" it goes right to "Approve the claim". From there "Claim queued" goes down into the Warehouse operator lane to "Book a courier", then "Inspect the item", then "Refund released" (green), the goal. "Approve the claim" also has a side arrow labelled "Logs" to the "Claims table".

## 3. Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

- "Damage clear in the photo?" has two exits. "Clear" runs along the bottom of the CS agent lane and up to "Approve the claim". "Unclear" runs up to "Send the second photo" in the Customer lane. From that shape a line runs down and back into the diamond's right side, so the question is asked again with the second photo.
- "Approve the claim" has two outgoing lines. "Logs" goes to "Claims table" and ends there. "Claim queued" goes to "Book a courier".

There is no exit from the diamond for a rejected claim. The board shows no way to refuse a claim.

- The "Clear" line leaves the diamond at the bottom and runs along the lane edge, so it looks as if it brushes the Warehouse operator lane. It is readable but crowded.
- The line from "Send the second photo" crosses the CS agent lane and comes into the diamond from the right, near the "Approve the claim" entry arrow. It takes a second look to tell the two apart.
- The footnote says every decision is logged in the claims table, but only "Approve the claim" has the Logs arrow.
- "Refund released" sits in the Warehouse operator lane, which could mean the warehouse releases the refund. That is probably not meant, but the board does not say who pays it out.
