## What decided the ranking

The ranking turns on the transcripts and the replies, not on the boards. The three boards are the same drawing at 22 of 22. The composer drew all of them from near-identical models, which differ only in the beta label (deepseek keeps "Beta, 5 percent of workspaces", glm and swe write "Beta for 5 percent") and in the source line wording. Each get_figjam read-back matches its own model label for label. Every bar sits on the dates the request implies (design 2 to 16 Nov, sync engine 2 to 23 Nov, build 16 to 30 Nov, integration 30 Nov to 7 Dec, beta 7 to 21 Dec, release 21 Dec).

glm ranks first because it kept the playback before the model, found the three same-title boards and proved with a before and after page diff that its compose added one section and changed nothing else, which its read-back line then states. deepseek ranks second because it wrote, checked and composed its model to a local file in turn 1 before the playback had an answer and then asserted "changed nothing else" without a read taken after the write. swe ranks third for two reasons. It never read the page before writing into a file it knew held content, so it never saw the three same-title boards. Its two stop replies also carry 17 em dashes and 5 semicolons, which the Human Voice card bans.

This order is judgment on conduct and not a measured score. It would flip with a different weighting. glm spent 84,359 output tokens and 375 s in turn 3 on two failed composes, so a grader who ranks efficiency first would put swe on top. A grader who counts the up-front duplicate warning above everything would put deepseek on top.
