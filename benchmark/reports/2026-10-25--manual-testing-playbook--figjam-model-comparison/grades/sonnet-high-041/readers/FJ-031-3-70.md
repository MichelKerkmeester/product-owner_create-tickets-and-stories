# Reader notes for FJ-031-3-70

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Start a return". The subtitle says it shows how a Fernhouse customer starts a return in the app, from the order list to a confirmed return. The question it answers is: what steps and choices does a customer go through in the app to get a return confirmed?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title at top left. Second my eye goes to the green goal box with the thick pink outline at the far right ("Return confirmed with QR code"), because it is the most saturated shape on the board. After that I take in the three tinted section frames that run left to right and the two yellow diamonds.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end, naming the shapes

The board runs left to right across three labelled frames.

In the frame "Find the order" a blue rounded box "Order list" (the start) leads by arrow to a white rounded box "Order detail".

An arrow carries the path into the frame "Request the return", where it reaches a yellow diamond "Within 30 days of delivery?". On Yes it goes to a white rounded box "Pick a reason", which leads to a second yellow diamond "Drop-off or pickup?".

Both exits of that diamond end in the frame "Done", at a green rounded box "Return confirmed with QR code" which has a thick pink outline.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

"Within 30 days of delivery?" has two exits. Yes runs right to "Pick a reason". No drops down from the bottom point of the diamond and runs right to an orange box with a dashed outline, "Request an exception", which stays inside the "Request the return" frame.

"Drop-off or pickup?" has two exits. Pickup runs straight right to "Return confirmed with QR code". Drop-off leaves the top point of the diamond, runs along above the frames and drops down into the top of the same goal box. So both choices end in the same confirmed return.

## 5 What the key says each colour, shape, outline and line style means, and whether every mark is in the key

The key at the bottom lists seven entries. A blue rounded box means where it starts. A white rounded box with a grey outline means a screen or step. A yellow diamond means a decision. A green rounded box with a green outline means goal reached. An orange box with a dashed orange outline means leaves the flow. A thin grey arrow means leads to. A green box with a thick pink outline means in focus.

Every node and arrow on the board matches a key entry. What is not in the key is the colour of the three section frames (light peach for "Find the order", light mint for "Request the return" and light cream for "Done"). They read as plain grouping backgrounds and do not seem to carry meaning, but the key does not say so. The branch labels on the lines (Yes, No, Drop-off and Pickup) are not in the key either, though they explain themselves.

## 6 Anything you could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous, with where it sits

All text is readable at both sizes.

"Request an exception" (bottom middle) is a dead end. The key calls it leaving the flow, but the board does not say what happens next, who reviews the exception or whether the customer can come back into the flow if it is granted.

The pink "In focus" outline sits on the goal box at the far right. The board does not say why that step is in focus or what a reader should look at there, so its purpose is unclear.

The Drop-off and Pickup exits both land on the same "Return confirmed with QR code" box. It is not clear whether a QR code is also issued for a pickup, or whether the confirmation differs between the two.

"Pick a reason" shows no list of reasons and no effect of the reason on the flow.

The arrow from "Order detail" into the first decision carries no label, so the action that starts the return (a button or link on the order detail screen) is not named.

The footnote at the bottom left says the 30 day exit was updated to "Request an exception" and that free pickup over 100 euro (a proposal under review) and orders not yet delivered were left out. That is clear, but it means the board does not cover what an undelivered order does.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Mostly yes for the happy path, since the steps, the two decisions and the shared end state are clear and keyed, but no for the full flow, because the "Request an exception" exit stops with no follow-up and the reason for the "In focus" highlight is not stated.
