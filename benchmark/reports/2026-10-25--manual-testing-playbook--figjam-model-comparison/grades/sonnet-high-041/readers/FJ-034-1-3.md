# FJ-034-1-3 fresh reader

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Damaged-item refund". The subtitle states the question: which team does each step of a damaged-item claim, from the customer's photo to the released refund.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the bold title and then the pink-outlined yellow diamond "Approve the claim?" in the middle lane. Its thick magenta outline is the loudest mark on the board. Second I see the three horizontal swimlanes (Customer, CS and Warehouse) and the row of white steps running left to right along the bottom to the green "Refund released" box.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end, naming the shapes

The board is a swimlane flowchart with three lanes stacked top to bottom: Customer (peach), CS (pale cyan) and Warehouse (pale yellow).

1. In the Customer lane a blue rounded rectangle "Damage reported with a photo" is the start.
2. An arrow runs right and down into the CS lane to the yellow diamond "Approve the claim?", which has the thick pink outline.
3. From the diamond the "Approved" exit runs down into the Warehouse lane to the white rounded rectangle "Book a pickup".
4. An arrow leads to the white rounded rectangle "Inspect the item".
5. An arrow leads to the green rounded rectangle "Refund released", which is the goal.

A side loop runs from the diamond up to the Customer lane step "Send a second photo" and back to the diamond.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

There is one decision, "Approve the claim?" in the CS lane. It has two labelled exits.

- "Second photo needed" leaves the top of the diamond and goes to "Send a second photo" in the Customer lane. From there an unlabelled arrow returns into the right side of the diamond, so the claim is judged again.
- "Approved" leaves the bottom of the diamond and goes to "Book a pickup" in the Warehouse lane, then on to "Inspect the item" and "Refund released".

There is no exit for a rejected claim. There is also no branch after "Inspect the item", so the board does not say what happens if the inspection finds no damage.

## 5 What the key says each colour, shape, outline and line style means, and whether every mark is in the key

The key at the bottom lists six entries.

- Blue rounded rectangle means where it starts.
- White rounded rectangle with a grey outline means step.
- Yellow diamond means decision.
- Green rounded rectangle with a green outline means goal reached.
- Thin grey line with an arrowhead means leads to.
- Yellow diamond with a thick pink outline means "In focus: the only branch".

Every node shape on the board matches a key entry. The marks not in the key are the three lane background colours (peach, cyan and yellow) and the small text labels on the arrows. Both are self-explaining because each lane carries its team name and each label sits on its own arrow. The plain yellow diamond in the key never appears on its own on the board, since the only diamond carries the pink focus outline. The footer reads "Source: the steps described in the request".

## 6 Anything you could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous, with where it sits

- The decision "Approve the claim?" in the CS lane reads as a yes or no question, but its exits are "Approved" and "Second photo needed". There is no "Rejected" or "No" exit, so I cannot tell whether a claim can ever be refused.
- The loop through "Send a second photo" has no limit shown. A claim could cycle between the customer and CS with no stated end.
- "Refund released" sits in the Warehouse lane at the bottom right. It is unclear whether the warehouse really releases money or whether finance or CS does it after inspection.
- No outcome is shown for an inspection that fails, between "Inspect the item" and "Refund released".
- The key phrase "In focus: the only branch" is a little odd. It tells me the pink outline marks the decision being highlighted but not why it is in focus.

Everything on the board was legible at 100 percent and the arrows were easy to follow.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for the happy path, because the lanes, steps and the single decision are clear and keyed, but a product manager would still need to ask what happens to a rejected claim or a failed inspection before building the full flow.
