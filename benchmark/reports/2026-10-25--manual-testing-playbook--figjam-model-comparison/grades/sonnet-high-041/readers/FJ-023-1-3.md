# Reader notes: FJ-023-1-3

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Damaged-item refund". The subtitle states the question: who handles a damaged-item claim, from the customer's report to the released refund. It is a swimlane flowchart with three lanes, Customer, CS agent and Warehouse.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title and then the pink "Approve the claim" box in the middle, which has the thickest and brightest outline on the board. Second the three coloured horizontal lanes and the green "Refund released" end box at the lower right. The key strip along the bottom comes after that.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

The flow starts in the Customer lane at the blue rounded box "Damage reported". An arrow runs down into the CS agent lane to the yellow diamond "Damage clear?". The "Clear" exit leaves the bottom of the diamond and goes to the pink rounded box "Approve the claim". From there an arrow labelled "To the warehouse queue" drops into the Warehouse lane to the white rounded box "Book a courier pickup", then to the white rounded box "Inspect the item", then to the green rounded box "Refund released", which is the goal. A side arrow labelled "Logs the decision" runs from "Approve the claim" to the purple cylinder "Claims table" in the CS agent lane.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

There is one decision, the diamond "Damage clear?" in the CS agent lane. Its "Unclear" exit leaves the top, goes up into the Customer lane and reaches the cyan parallelogram "Send a second photo". From that parallelogram an unlabelled arrow returns down into the CS agent lane and back into the right side of the diamond, so the check repeats. Its "Clear" exit goes to "Approve the claim" as described above. "Approve the claim" also branches to two places at once: the "Logs the decision" arrow to the Claims table and the "To the warehouse queue" arrow to the courier pickup. There are no other branches.

## 5 What the key says

The key lists a blue rounded box as "Where it starts", a white rounded box as "Step", a cyan parallelogram as "Input or output", a yellow diamond as "Decision", a green rounded box as "Goal reached", a purple cylinder as "Data store", a thin grey arrow as "Leads to" and a pink box with a thick magenta outline as "In focus". Every shape on the board matches a key entry. The three lane background colours (peach, pale mint and pale cream) are not in the key, but the lane labels name them so they need no entry. The text labels on the arrows are not explained in the key but read on their own. Only one line style is used and it is in the key.

## 6 Anything I could not read, follow or found ambiguous

All text is readable at both zoom levels. The open points are about meaning, not legibility.

- The decision "Damage clear?" has no exit for a claim that is clearly not damaged or should be rejected. Only Unclear and Clear exist, so a rejected claim has no path on the board.
- The Unclear loop through "Send a second photo" has no limit or timeout, so it is not clear what happens if the customer never sends a photo or the second photo is still unclear.
- "Inspect the item" in the Warehouse lane has a single exit straight to "Refund released". There is no outcome for an inspection that finds no damage.
- "Refund released" sits in the Warehouse lane, which suggests the warehouse releases the money. That may be intended but it is unusual and not explained.
- The footnote says "Every decision is logged in the claims table, drawn from the approval". Only the approval is drawn as logged, so it is unclear whether the Unclear outcome of the diamond is also logged.
- The board does not say why "Approve the claim" is the step in focus.
- The footnote notes that pickup is skipped under 20 euros as a pilot that is not live, which is clear, but it is easy to miss in the small grey text below the key.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

No, because the happy path and the photo loop are clear but there is no rejection path, no failed-inspection outcome and no limit on the photo loop, so a product manager would have to ask how those cases end.
