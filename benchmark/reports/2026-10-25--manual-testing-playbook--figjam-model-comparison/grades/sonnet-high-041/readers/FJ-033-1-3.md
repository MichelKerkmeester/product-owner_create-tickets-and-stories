# Fresh reader notes: FJ-033-1-3

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "How Loomlist block sync works". The subtitle says it shows how the desktop, web and iOS apps share block changes through the sync platform, and how that platform wakes sleeping iPhones through Apple's push service. So the question is: which parts are involved when a block changes, and how does the change travel between them?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the big bold title at the top left. Next my eye goes to the box in the middle with a thick pink outline, the Sync platform, because it is the only strongly highlighted shape. After that I see the row of three green app pills on the left and the purple cylinder on the right.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

The board reads left to right in five labelled groups. Apps holds three rounded green pills: Desktop app, Web app and iOS app. Each sends a solid arrow labelled "Syncs blocks" into Entry point, a rounded blue pill in the Entry group. The desktop arrow comes in from the top and the iOS arrow from the bottom. Entry point sends a solid arrow labelled "Routes changes" to Sync platform, a white rounded box with a pink outline in the Platform group. Sync platform sends a solid arrow labelled "Stores every change" to Change store, a purple cylinder in the Stores group. Sync platform also sends a dashed arrow downward and then right, labelled "Apple push: Wakes sleeping iPhones", to Apple push service, a grey pill with a dashed outline in the Outside group.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

This is a system map, not a flow with decisions, so there are no yes or no branches. The relationships are these. Three apps all feed one Entry point. Entry point feeds the Sync platform. The Sync platform fans out to two places: the Change store, where it saves every change with a direct call, and Apple push service, which it calls asynchronously to wake sleeping iPhones. There is no timing or ordering shown, and the footer says so: "Left out: the order of one sync".

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in the key

The key at the bottom says: green rounded pill means App or client. Blue pill with a bright blue outline means Edge or gateway. White rounded box means Service. Purple cylinder means Data store. Grey pill with a dashed outline means Outside system. A solid arrow means Leads to. A dashed arrow means Async or outside call. A thick pink outline means In focus.

Every node and line on the board matches a key entry. Sync platform is a white Service box with the pink In focus outline on top, which reads fine. The only marks not in the key are the five soft tinted group boxes (Apps, Entry, Platform, Stores and Outside). Their background colours are not explained, but their labels make them clear enough.

## 6 Anything I could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous

All text was readable at both zooms. My open questions are about meaning, not legibility. There are no arrows going back to the apps, so I cannot tell how the web and desktop apps receive changes made on another device. Only iPhones get a wake-up, and the board does not show what the phone does after it wakes. The footer says "Entry point added for the layout", which makes me unsure whether Entry point is a real component or just a drawing aid. The footer also says the source was a leadership update request with no doc supplied, so I do not know how verified the picture is.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for a high level understanding, because the parts and their connections are clear and fully keyed, but no for any decision about sync behaviour, since the return path to other devices, the order of a sync and the status of the Entry point all need someone to confirm.
