# Reader notes: FJ-036 1-3

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Change booking dates". The subtitle says it shows how a Roamstay guest changes the dates of a booking in the app, from the booking page to the updated booking. The question it answers is: what steps and decisions does a guest go through to move a booking to new dates, and what happens to the price?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title at top left. Second the pink outlined yellow diamond "How does the new price compare?" in the right hand section, because its thick magenta outline is the loudest mark on the board. After that the eye picks up the blue "Booking page" start on the left and the green "Updated booking shown" end on the right, which frame the flow.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

The board reads left to right across two tinted sections. The left peach section is labelled "Choose the dates" and the right mint section is labelled "Settle the price".

Main path: blue rounded rectangle "Booking page", arrow labelled "Tap Change dates" to white rounded rectangle "Pick new dates", arrow to yellow diamond "Room free on those dates?". The "Yes" exit leaves the top of that diamond, runs over the top and into the left point of the pink outlined yellow diamond "How does the new price compare?" in the second section. From there the guest reaches one of three routes that all end at the green rounded rectangle "Updated booking shown".

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

Decision 1, "Room free on those dates?":
- Yes: goes straight to the price decision "How does the new price compare?".
- No: goes right to the white step "Other rooms shown". From its bottom a line labelled "Pick a room" runs right and up into the lower left edge of the price decision. So both exits of the first decision rejoin at the price decision.

Decision 2, "How does the new price compare?":
- Lower: exits the top and goes to the white step "Difference returned as credit", which then leads down into the top of "Updated booking shown".
- Higher: exits the right point to the white step "Pay the difference", which leads right into "Updated booking shown".
- Same, nothing charged: exits the bottom, runs along under the section and rises into the bottom of "Updated booking shown".

There are no loops and only one end point.

## 5 What the key says and whether every mark is in it

The key at the bottom lists six items:
- Light blue rounded rectangle with blue outline: Where it starts.
- White rounded rectangle with grey outline: Screen or step.
- Yellow diamond: Decision.
- Light green rounded rectangle with green outline: Goal reached.
- Thin grey arrow: Leads to.
- Yellow diamond with thick magenta outline: In focus.

Every node on the board matches a key entry. All connectors are the same solid grey arrow style, so one line entry covers them. The two tinted section backgrounds (peach and mint) are not in the key, but each carries its own heading so they read as stage groupings without needing one. The small text labels on lines (Yes, No, Pick a room, Lower, Higher, Same, nothing charged, Tap Change dates) are plain labels and need no key entry. A footnote under the key lists the source and what was left out: payment failure, backing out and booking rules such as cancellation windows.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

- All text is readable at 100% and in the overview.
- "In focus" is defined in the key but the board never says why the price decision is the one in focus. A reader has to guess that it is the part under discussion.
- The "Yes" line from the first diamond leaves from its top and lands on the left point of the price diamond, while the "Pick a room" line lands on the lower left edge just below it. The two arrival points are close together at the left of the price diamond and take a second look to separate.
- The "Lower" exit leaves from the top point of the price diamond, close to where the long "Yes" line passes overhead. They do not cross but the area is busy.
- "Other rooms shown" is ambiguous about whether those rooms are in the same property and whether picking one changes the price comparison basis. There is also no exit for a guest who picks no room, though the footnote says backing out is left out on purpose.
- "Difference returned as credit" does not say whether credit means app credit or a refund to the card.
- "Pay the difference" has a single exit to success. The footnote explains that payment failure is out of scope.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, because the path, both decisions and every exit are labelled and lead to a single clear end point, and the footnote states the left out cases, though they may still ask what "In focus" is meant to flag and what kind of credit a lower price gives.
