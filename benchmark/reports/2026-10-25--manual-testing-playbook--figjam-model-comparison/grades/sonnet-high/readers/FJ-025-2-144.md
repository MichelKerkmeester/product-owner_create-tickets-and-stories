# Reader notes: FJ-025 board 2

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "2. Checkout payment, card declined". The subtitle says it shows the calls a Roamstay checkout makes in order when the card is declined, for the product and engineering team. The question it answers is: when a guest's card is declined at checkout, which systems talk to each other, in what order, and what does the guest app get back?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title at the top left. Second the one pink dashed arrow near the bottom of the diagram, labelled "402 Card declined", because it is the only coloured line on an otherwise grey board. After that the eye goes to the three rounded participant boxes across the top and the purple numbered circles running down the middle.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

This is a sequence diagram with three participants, each a rounded box at the top with a grey dashed lifeline running down to a matching smaller rounded box at the bottom: GuestApp on the left, BookingApi in the middle and PaymentProvider on the right. Read top to bottom by the purple step numbers:

1. GuestApp to BookingApi, solid arrow: "Post the booking".
2. BookingApi to itself, solid self-loop arrow: "Hold the room".
3. BookingApi to PaymentProvider, solid arrow: "Authorise card".
4. PaymentProvider back to BookingApi, grey dashed arrow: "Declined".
5. BookingApi to itself, solid self-loop arrow: "Release the hold".
6. BookingApi back to GuestApp, pink dashed arrow: "402 Card declined".

The story ends with the guest app holding a 402 error and the room hold released.

## 4 Decisions, branches, relationships, messages and timing

There are no decision shapes and no branches. The board shows a single path, the declined case only. The relationships are the messages listed above: two requests out (steps 1 and 3), two internal actions on BookingApi (steps 2 and 5, drawn as self-loops) and two replies back (steps 4 and 6). Timing is shown only by vertical order and the step numbers. There are no durations, timeouts or retries. The footer says the authorised card case is left out and is drawn on board 1.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in the key

The key, in a light grey box under the diagram, lists:

- Rounded white box: Participant.
- Solid grey line with arrowhead: Request.
- Dashed grey line with arrowhead: Reply.
- Purple circle with a number: Step number.
- Thick solid pink arrow: In focus.

Almost every mark is covered. Three small gaps:

- The in-focus sample in the key is a solid pink line, while the in-focus message on the board (step 6) is a dashed pink line. I read it as "a reply that is in focus", which works, but the key does not show that pink can be combined with the dashed reply style.
- The grey dashed vertical lifelines and the smaller participant boxes repeated at the bottom are not in the key. They are standard sequence diagram conventions, so a reader who knows the form will not miss them, but they are not explained.
- The self-loop arrows on BookingApi (steps 2 and 5) are drawn in the request style but the key does not say what a message from a participant to itself means. I read them as internal actions.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

All text is readable at both zoom levels. Points of ambiguity:

- Step 4 has its number circle on the PaymentProvider side, to the right of the lifeline, while the other circles sit next to the lifeline the message starts from. That is consistent (the reply starts at PaymentProvider) but it means the numbers zig-zag between columns, which is a small reading bump.
- The board does not say why step 6 is the one in focus. I assume it is the guest-facing outcome, but the board never states it.
- "Hold the room" and "Release the hold" are internal to BookingApi. The board does not say whether these call a separate inventory system or how long the hold lasts.
- The footer says "the payments brief, as summarised in the request", which points to a source I cannot see from this board.
- It is not shown what the guest sees on screen after the 402, only the HTTP status.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, for understanding the declined path, because the six numbered steps, the participants and the 402 outcome are clear and the key covers nearly every mark, but they would need to ask what the guest is shown after the 402 and why step 6 is singled out if they wanted to change the experience.
