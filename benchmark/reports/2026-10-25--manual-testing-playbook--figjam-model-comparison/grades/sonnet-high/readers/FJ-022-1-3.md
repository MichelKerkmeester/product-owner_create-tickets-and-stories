# Reader notes for FJ-022-1-3

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Start a return". The subtitle says the board shows how a Fernhouse customer starts a return in the app, from the order list to a confirmed return. The question it answers is: what steps and checks does a customer pass through in the app to get a return confirmed?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title at top left and then the pink outlined green box at the far right, "Confirmation with QR code", which is the strongest mark on the board. Second I see the two yellow diamonds in the middle section, which tell me there are two decisions on the way.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end, naming the shapes

The board reads left to right across three labelled sections. In "Find the order" a blue rounded box "Order list" (the start) leads to a white rounded box "Order detail". An arrow crosses into "Request the return" and reaches the yellow diamond "Within 30 days of delivery?". Its right exit, labelled "Tap Start a return", leads to the white rounded box "Pick a return reason". That box leads to the second yellow diamond "Drop-off point or courier pickup?". Both of its exits lead into the "Done" section and end at the green rounded box "Confirmation with QR code", outlined in pink as in focus.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

The first decision, "Within 30 days of delivery?", has two exits. The right exit is labelled "Tap Start a return" and goes to "Pick a return reason". The bottom exit is labelled "No" and drops down then right to the orange box "Contact support", which has no arrow out, so it is where the customer leaves the flow.

The second decision, "Drop-off point or courier pickup?", has two exits. The top exit, labelled "Drop-off point", rises above the diamond, runs right along the top of the board and comes down into "Confirmation with QR code". The right exit, labelled "Courier pickup", runs straight into the same "Confirmation with QR code" box. Both choices end at the same screen.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in the key

The key at the bottom lists six marks. A blue rounded box means where it starts. A white rounded box means a screen or step. A yellow diamond means a decision. A green rounded box means goal reached. An orange rounded box means leaves the flow. A green box with a thick pink outline means in focus.

Every node on the board matches a key entry. Not in the key are the three section backgrounds (peach for "Find the order" and pale mint for "Request the return" and "Done") and the connector lines. All connectors are the same thin grey solid arrow, so there is only one line style and its meaning (next step) is easy to infer, but the key does not state it. A footnote under the key says the sources were the app and the returns doc and that free pickup for orders over 100 euros was left out because it is a proposal under review.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

The connector labels ("Tap Start a return", "No", "Drop-off point", "Courier pickup") are small and light grey and need zooming to read at overview size. The first decision mixes label styles: the negative exit says "No" but the positive exit says "Tap Start a return" instead of "Yes", so the reader has to infer that the right exit means yes. The second decision has two exits that land on the same box, so the board does not show whether drop-off and courier pickup lead to different confirmations or extra steps (for example choosing a pickup slot or a drop-off location). The "Drop-off point" connector leaves the "Request the return" section along the very top edge, close to the section border, which makes it look slightly detached at first glance. "Contact support" is a dead end with no detail on what happens next. The "Order detail" to decision arrow carries no label, so the action that triggers the 30 day check (presumably tapping a return button) is not named.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, mostly, because the path, the single eligibility check, the exit to support and the shared end screen are all clear and keyed, though a product manager would likely still ask whether drop-off and courier pickup really need no extra steps before the confirmation.
