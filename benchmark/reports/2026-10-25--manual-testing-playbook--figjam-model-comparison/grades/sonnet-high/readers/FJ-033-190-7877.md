# Fresh reader notes: How block sync works

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "How block sync works". The subtitle reads "How the desktop, web and iOS apps stay in step through the sync platform, for the leadership update". The question it answers is which parts a change passes through when one of the three apps edits a block, and where that change ends up.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title at top left. Second the Sync Platform box in the middle, because it is the only shape with a thick pink outline and it pulls the eye away from the pastel boxes around it. After that the eye runs left to right along the solid arrows.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

This is an architecture or system diagram read left to right in five labelled containers.

- Apps container (light blue panel): three teal rounded boxes stacked vertically, Desktop App, Web App and iOS App.
- Each app has a solid arrow labelled "Sends changes" into Entry Point. Desktop enters it from above, Web from the left and iOS from below.
- Entry container (peach panel): one blue rounded box, Entry Point.
- A solid arrow labelled "Routes sync requests" goes from Entry Point to Sync Platform.
- Platform container (mint panel): one white rounded box with a thick pink border, Sync Platform.
- A solid arrow labelled "Stores every change" goes from Sync Platform to Change Store.
- Stored changes container (yellow panel): one purple cylinder, Change Store.
- A dashed arrow leaves the bottom of Sync Platform, runs along the bottom and ends at Apple Push Service. Its label is "Apple: Wakes sleeping iPhones".
- Apple container (lavender panel): one grey rounded box, Apple Push Service.

A footnote under the key reads "Source: product team description of block sync. Entry point is assumed. Left out: Apple's hop to the phone, any internal parts".

## 4 Decisions, branches and relationships

There are no decision shapes and no yes or no branches. The relationships are:

- Three apps fan in to a single Entry Point, all with the same "Sends changes" message.
- Entry Point forwards to Sync Platform ("Routes sync requests").
- Sync Platform fans out two ways. A synchronous solid write to Change Store ("Stores every change") and an async dashed call to Apple Push Service ("Wakes sleeping iPhones").

No timing, ordering numbers or conditions are shown. The board does not say whether the store write happens before the push or whether both happen for every change.

## 5 What the key says and whether every mark is in it

The key, in a grey panel at the bottom, lists:

- Teal rounded box: App or client.
- Blue rounded box: Edge or gateway.
- White rounded box: Service.
- Purple cylinder: Data store.
- Grey rounded box: Outside system.
- Dashed arrow: Async or outside call.
- Thick pink outline: In focus.

Every node on the board matches a key entry. Sync Platform combines two entries (white Service fill plus pink In focus outline), which reads fine. Two marks are not in the key. The solid arrow has no entry, so a reader must infer it means a direct or synchronous call by contrast with the dashed one. The five tinted container panels (light blue, peach, mint, yellow and lavender) are also not in the key, though their headings name them so they read as grouping rather than meaning.

## 6 Anything unreadable, unclear or ambiguous

- All text is legible at 100 percent. Nothing is cut off or overlapping.
- The subtitle promises the apps "stay in step" but every arrow points away from the apps. There is no return path showing how Web or Desktop receive another app's change, and only iPhones are woken. This gap sits on the left half of the board and is the main thing I could not follow.
- The dashed edge label starts with "Apple:" which repeats the target container name and reads oddly (bottom middle, between Sync Platform and Apple Push Service).
- The footnote says the Entry Point is assumed, so the blue box in the Entry container is a guess rather than a confirmed part.
- Solid arrow meaning is missing from the key (see section 5).
- Each container holds a single node, so the Entry, Platform, Stored changes and Apple panels add colour without adding grouping information.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

No, because although the inbound path from the three apps to storage and the iPhone wake call are clear, the board never shows how changes travel back to the other apps, which is the very "stay in step" behaviour the subtitle promises, and the Entry Point is flagged as assumed.
