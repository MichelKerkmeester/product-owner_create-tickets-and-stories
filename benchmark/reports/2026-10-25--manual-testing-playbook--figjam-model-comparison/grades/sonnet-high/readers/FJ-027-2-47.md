# Fresh reader notes: Stays model

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Stays model" and the subtitle reads "The tables behind a stay, with the guest tables apart from the partner tables". The board answers the question: which database tables sit behind a stay, and how do they connect to each other, with Booking as the table at the centre.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title and then the Booking box, because it is the only box with a thick pink border. Second I see the two coloured zones, a light blue "Guest side" band across the top and a light orange "Partner side" block at the bottom left. After that I notice the lines that run from the partner tables up into Booking.

## 3 The main structure from start to end, naming the shapes

This is an entity relationship diagram, not a flow, so there is no start or end. It has six white rectangles, each one a table with its keys listed inside.

- Guest side (blue zone): Guest (PK id), Booking (PK id, FK guestId, FK roomId, FK ratePlanId) and Payment (PK id, FK bookingId).
- Partner side (orange zone): Property (PK id), Room (PK id, FK propertyId) and Rate Plan (PK id, FK propertyId).

Reading left to right on the guest side: Guest makes Booking, and Booking holds Payment. On the partner side, Property sits at the top left and feeds Room and Rate Plan. Room and Rate Plan then each send a long line out of the orange zone and up into Booking. Below the diagram is a grey Key panel and a source line at the very bottom.

## 4 The relationships it shows

There are no decisions or branches. Every line is labelled with a verb and the cardinality "1 to 0..n", and the line ends use crow's foot marks (two bars on the "one" end, a circle and fork on the "zero or many" end).

- Guest "makes (1 to 0..n)" Booking: one guest has zero or many bookings. The line runs from the right edge of Guest along the top of the blue zone and drops into the top of Booking.
- Booking "holds (1 to 0..n)" Payment: one booking has zero or many payments.
- Property "offers (1 to 0..n)" Room: one property has zero or many rooms.
- Property "publishes (1 to 0..n)" Rate Plan: one property has zero or many rate plans. The line leaves the bottom of Property and turns right into Rate Plan.
- Room "is reserved by (1 to 0..n)" Booking: one room has zero or many bookings. The line leaves the right of Room, rises and enters the left edge of Booking.
- Rate Plan "prices (1 to 0..n)" Booking: one rate plan prices zero or many bookings. The line leaves the right of Rate Plan, runs right and rises into the bottom of Booking.

The foreign keys in each box match these lines: Booking carries guestId, roomId and ratePlanId. Payment carries bookingId. Room and Rate Plan both carry propertyId.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in the key

The key lists three things:

- A plain white rectangle with a grey border means "Entity".
- A solid grey line means "Identifying".
- A white rectangle with a thick pink border means "In focus".

Not every mark is in the key. These are missing:

- The crow's foot end marks (double bar, circle and fork). The "1 to 0..n" text on each line covers their meaning, but the key never explains the notation.
- The blue "Guest side" zone and the orange "Partner side" zone. Their headings say what they are, but the colours themselves are not in the key.
- The "PK" and "FK" prefixes and the trailing word "key" on each field.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

- Key, middle entry: "Identifying" is the only line style, so every relationship on the board claims to be identifying. Yet each child table has its own PK id, which usually signals a non identifying relationship. There is no non identifying style to compare with, so I cannot tell whether the word means something specific or is just a name for "relationship line".
- Below Booking, between the two vertical lines: the label "is reserved by (1 to 0..n)" sits between the Room line and the Rate Plan line, so at a glance it could belong to either. Following the line from Room shows it belongs to the Room line, but it took tracing.
- The "is reserved by" label reads in the passive voice from Room toward Booking, while the other labels read active from the parent ("makes", "holds", "offers"). It is understandable but reads backwards compared with the rest.
- Booking sits inside the Guest side zone even though three of its four keys point at partner tables. The board does not say why Booking counts as a guest table.
- Each field ends with the word "key", which adds nothing because the PK or FK prefix already says so.
- Footer: "Source: the stays schema note, as summarised in the request. Left out: every column except the keys." I do not know which schema note or request this means, and I cannot see any column beyond the keys.
- Everything else is crisp and readable at 100 percent. No text is cut off or overlapping.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for understanding: a product manager can see which six tables make up a stay and how a booking ties a guest to a room, a rate plan and its payments, but they would need to ask about the "Identifying" line meaning and the missing non key columns before using it for any schema or scoping decision.
