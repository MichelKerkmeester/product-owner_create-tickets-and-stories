# Reader notes: FJ-019 board 2.99

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "2. Push delivery". The subtitle states the question: how does a block change become a push notification, travelling from sync-service through the change stream and the push worker to Apple's push service. The "2." and a footnote reference to "board 1" tell me this is the second board in a set.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title and then the Push worker box, because it has a thick bright pink border that no other node has. Second I see the four tinted group frames across the middle (Entry, Services, Events, Outside) and the chain of labelled arrows linking them. The key along the bottom and the small sources footnote come last.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

This is an architecture diagram that reads from left to right with one loop back.

- The API gateway is a blue rounded box in the Entry frame. A solid arrow labelled "Routes sync requests" goes to sync-service.
- sync-service is a white rounded box at the top of the Services frame. A dashed arrow labelled "Produces block changes" goes right to the Change stream.
- The Change stream is an orange cylinder in the teal Events frame. A dashed line leaves its bottom, turns left and arrives at the Push worker with the label "Consumes block changes".
- The Push worker is a white rounded box with a pink border at the bottom of the Services frame. A dashed line leaves its bottom, runs right under the frames and turns up into the Apple push service with the label "Apple Push: Sends notifications".
- The Apple push service is a grey rounded box in the yellow Outside frame. This is the end of the path.

## 4 Decisions, branches and relationships

There are no decisions or branches. The path is one straight chain of four links: the gateway routes requests to sync-service, sync-service produces changes into the stream, the push worker consumes changes from the stream and the push worker sends notifications to Apple's push service. Only the first link is a solid line. The board calls the other three async or outside calls. No timing, retries or failure paths are shown, and the footnote says retries, auth, the clients and stores from board 1 and delivery from Apple to the iOS device are left out on purpose. It also says the calls are assumed and the source is the team's description of the block sync.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in it

The key lists six items:

- A blue rounded box means edge or gateway.
- A white rounded box means a service.
- An orange cylinder means a queue or stream.
- A grey rounded box means an outside system.
- A dashed arrow means an async or outside call.
- A pink-bordered box means in focus.

Not every mark is in the key. The solid arrow from the API gateway to sync-service has no key entry, so I have to infer that solid means a synchronous call. The tinted group frames (peach for Entry and Services, teal for Events, yellow for Outside) are not in the key either, though their titles make them easy to read.

## 6 What I could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous

- Push worker (bottom of the Services frame): the pink "in focus" border tells me it matters, but nothing on the board says why it is the focus or what question about it a reader should take away.
- Bottom connector to the Apple push service: the label starts "Apple Push:" which repeats the target name and reads like a protocol or channel name. It is unclear whether that prefix means something different from the rest of the label.
- The solid arrow between the API gateway and sync-service is the only solid line, and its meaning is inferred, not stated.
- The footnote says the calls are assumed, so I cannot tell which links are confirmed and which are guesses.

All text was legible at both zoom levels.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for understanding the flow, because the four-step path from gateway to Apple's push service is clear and labelled, but a product manager would still need to ask why the push worker is in focus and which of the assumed calls are confirmed before acting on it.
