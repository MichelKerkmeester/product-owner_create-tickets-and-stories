# FJ-025-1-3 fresh reader

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "1. Checkout payment, card authorised". The subtitle says it shows the calls a Roamstay checkout makes in order when the card is authorised, for the product and engineering team. The question it answers is: in the happy path where the card goes through, which system calls which and in what order?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title at the top left. Second the single pink arrow labelled "Authorise card" in the middle of the diagram, because it is the only coloured line and is thicker than the rest. After that the purple numbered circles pull the eye down the steps in order.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

This is a sequence diagram with four participants drawn as rounded rectangles across the top (GuestApp, BookingApi, PaymentProvider and PartnerHub) and repeated as smaller rounded rectangles at the bottom. Each has a grey dashed lifeline running down between the two boxes.

The steps, read top to bottom:

1. GuestApp to BookingApi, solid request arrow "Post the booking".
2. BookingApi to itself, solid self-call loop "Hold the room".
3. BookingApi to PaymentProvider, thick pink solid arrow "Authorise card".
4. PaymentProvider back to BookingApi, dashed reply arrow "Authorised".
5. BookingApi to itself, solid self-call loop "Confirm the hold".
6. BookingApi to PartnerHub, solid request arrow "Tell the partner hub". It passes across the PaymentProvider lifeline.
7. BookingApi back to GuestApp, dashed reply arrow "201 Created".

The flow ends with GuestApp receiving 201 Created.

## 4 Decisions, branches, relationships, messages or timing

There are no decisions or branches on this board. It is a single linear happy path. The footer says the declined card case is left out and drawn on board 2. The timing is strictly top to bottom with seven numbered messages. BookingApi is the hub: it receives the booking, holds the room, calls the payment provider, confirms the hold, notifies the partner hub and only then answers the guest app. Only the payment call gets a drawn reply. The call to PartnerHub has no reply drawn.

## 5 What the key says

The key at the bottom lists five items: a white rounded rectangle means Participant, a thin solid grey arrow means Request, a dashed grey arrow means Reply, a purple numbered circle means Step number and a thick pink arrow means In focus. Almost every mark is covered. The self-call loops in steps 2 and 5 are not a separate key entry but use the Request line style, so they read fine. The grey dashed lifelines are not in the key, though they are standard for this diagram type. The bottom participant boxes are smaller than the top ones and the key shows only one size, which is a small mismatch but not confusing. The key does not say why "Authorise card" is the step in focus.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

All text is legible at 100 percent and the overview matches it. A few small points:

- Step number placement is not consistent. Steps 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 sit to the left of the sending lifeline, but step 4 sits to the right of the PaymentProvider lifeline and step 7 sits to the right of the BookingApi lifeline. It still reads correctly but the eye has to hunt for 4 and 7.
- Step 6 "Tell the partner hub" has no reply. It is unclear whether this call is fire and forget or whether BookingApi waits for PartnerHub before sending 201 Created in step 7.
- The pink "In focus" highlight on step 3 has no stated reason. A reader can guess it is the payment call the board is about, but the board does not say so.
- "Hold the room" and "Confirm the hold" are drawn as internal BookingApi work. It is not stated whether they touch any other system such as inventory.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes, a product manager could read the full authorised checkout sequence and its owner systems without help, though they would likely ask whether the partner hub call is awaited before the guest gets 201 Created and would need board 2 for the declined case.
