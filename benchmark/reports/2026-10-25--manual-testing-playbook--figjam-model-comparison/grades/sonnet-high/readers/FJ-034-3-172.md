# Reader notes: FJ-034-3-172

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "Damaged-item refund". The subtitle states the question: who handles a damaged-item claim, from the customer's photo report to the released refund. It is a swimlane flow with three lanes: Customer on top, CS in the middle and Warehouse at the bottom.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title and then the bright pink "Approve the claim" box in the CS lane, because its thick magenta border is the loudest mark on the board. Second I see the yellow diamond "Photo shows the damage?" next to it, then the blue start box at top left and the green "Refund released" box at bottom right, which give the start and end of the story.

## 3 The main path from start to end

The flow starts in the Customer lane with the blue rounded box "Report damage with a photo". An arrow goes down into the CS lane to the yellow diamond "Photo shows the damage?". The "Yes" exit leaves the bottom of the diamond and runs right to the pink rounded box "Approve the claim" in the CS lane. From there an arrow drops into the Warehouse lane to the white rounded box "Book a pickup", then right to the white box "Inspect the item" and then right to the green rounded box "Refund released", which is the end.

## 4 Every decision and where each exit goes

There is one decision, the diamond "Photo shows the damage?" in the CS lane.

The exit labelled "Yes" leaves the bottom of the diamond, dips briefly below the CS lane and comes back up into "Approve the claim".

The exit labelled "Second photo" leaves the top of the diamond and goes up into the Customer lane to the white box "Send a second photo". From that box an arrow runs back down into the CS lane and enters the right side of the diamond, so the check is repeated. This is a loop.

The second exit is not labelled "No". I read "Second photo" as meaning the photo did not show the damage, but that is inference. There is no exit for the case where the second photo still does not show the damage, so as drawn the loop can repeat forever. The footnote says a decline path was left out on purpose. "Inspect the item" has only one exit, so the board does not say what happens if the inspection finds no damage.

## 5 What the key says and whether every mark is in it

The key shows five entries. Blue rounded box means "Where it starts". White rounded box with a grey border means "Step". Yellow diamond means "Decision". Green rounded box means "Goal reached". Pink box with a thick magenta border means "In focus".

Every node on the board matches a key entry. "Approve the claim" uses the "In focus" style, so it is a step that is highlighted, though the key does not say why it is in focus. The three lane background colours (peach, pale cyan and cream) are not in the key, but each lane is named by its label so they read fine. All connectors are the same thin grey solid line with an arrowhead, so there is only one line style and the key does not need to explain it. The edge labels "Yes" and "Second photo" are plain text on the lines.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

All text is readable at both zoom levels.

The "Second photo" label on the top exit of the diamond is a step name rather than an answer to the question, so the reader has to infer it is the "No" branch.

The loop through "Send a second photo" has no limit and no failure exit, and the footnote confirms the decline path is missing.

"Refund released" sits in the Warehouse lane, which suggests the warehouse releases the money. That may be correct, but it is a surprising owner and I cannot tell if finance or CS should own it.

The "In focus" highlight on "Approve the claim" has no stated reason on the board.

The "Yes" line and the "Approve the claim" to "Book a pickup" line both pass through the white gap between lanes, which is a little untidy but easy to follow.

The footnote at the bottom says "Source: the request in this conversation", which means nothing to someone opening the board later.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

No, because the happy path is clear but the board leaves out what happens when the photo still fails or the inspection fails, does not say why approval is in focus and puts the refund release with the warehouse without explanation, so a product manager would need to ask about those gaps before acting.
