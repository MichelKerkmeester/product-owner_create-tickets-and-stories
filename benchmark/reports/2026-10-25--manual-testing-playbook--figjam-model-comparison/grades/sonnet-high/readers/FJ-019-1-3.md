# Fresh reader notes: FJ-019 board 1 of 3

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "1. Block sync". The subtitle says it shows how a block change travels from the Desktop, Web and iOS clients through sync-service to the change stream, with the calls drawn from the team's description. The question it answers is which components a block change passes through and what each one calls on the way.

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First the large bold title and then the sync-service node, because it is the only shape with a thick magenta outline and it sits in the middle of the board. Second the left to right row of tinted groups (Clients, Entry, Services, Events, Stores) and the labelled lines that join them. The Key strip along the bottom and the small sources line under it come last.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end

This is an architecture or service map read left to right, not a flowchart.

- Start: three teal rounded rectangles in the Clients group. They are Desktop client, Web client and iOS client.
- Each client has a solid arrow labelled "Sends sync requests" into the blue rounded rectangle API gateway in the Entry group. Desktop enters from the top, Web from the left and iOS from the bottom.
- API gateway has a solid arrow labelled "Routes sync requests" into sync-service, a white rounded rectangle with a magenta focus outline in the Services group.
- sync-service has a dashed arrow labelled "Produces block changes" into Change stream, an orange horizontal cylinder in the Events group. This is where the subtitle says the path ends.
- sync-service also has two solid arrows to the Stores group. One leaves from its top, runs over the Events group and is labelled "Reads and writes blocks" into Blocks database, a purple vertical cylinder. The other leaves from its bottom and is labelled "Stores and fetches files" into File storage, a second purple cylinder.

## 4 Decisions, branches and relationships

There are no decisions or branch points. sync-service fans out to three targets: Change stream (asynchronous, dashed), Blocks database (solid) and File storage (solid). The three clients fan in to the API gateway. Every line carries a short verb label and every arrowhead points away from the clients, so the board shows who calls whom but not responses, timing or order between the three calls from sync-service.

## 5 What the key says, and whether every mark is in it

- Teal rounded rectangle: App or client.
- Blue rounded rectangle: Edge or gateway.
- White rounded rectangle: Service.
- Orange horizontal cylinder: Queue or stream.
- Purple vertical cylinder: Data store.
- Dashed arrow: Async or outside call.
- Thick magenta outline: In focus.

Almost every mark is covered. Gaps: the solid arrow is not in the key, so a reader has to infer it means a synchronous call by contrast with the dashed one. The tinted group boxes (light blue Clients and Stores, peach Entry, mint Services, cream Events) and their colours are not explained in the key, though their titles make them readable.

## 6 Anything unreadable, hard to follow or ambiguous

- Top right: the "Reads and writes blocks" line describes two way traffic but has a single arrowhead into Blocks database. "Stores and fetches files" at the bottom has the same issue.
- Top right: the "Reads and writes blocks" line runs above and past the Events group before dropping into Blocks database, so at a glance it can look like it starts from Change stream. Tracing it back shows it leaves the top of sync-service.
- Events group: nothing reads from Change stream. The subtitle stops there, but a reader may still ask who consumes the block changes and how other clients learn about them.
- Footer: the sources line lists "delivery from Apple's push service to the iOS device" as left out, yet no push service appears anywhere on the board, which hints at a step after the change stream that is not drawn. The same line says the calls are assumed, so the arrows are not confirmed against the real system.
- All text is legible at 100 percent. The line labels are small but clear.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Yes for understanding the write path, because every node and call is labelled and keyed, but no for the full sync story, because the calls are marked as assumed and the board stops at the change stream without showing who consumes it or how changes reach other devices.
