# Reader notes for FJ-029-2-60

## 1 Title and the question this board answers

The title is "1. Sign up". The subtitle says it shows how a new Loomlist user goes from the landing page to a verified email, for the product and design teams. The question it answers is: what steps does a new user take to sign up and get their email verified, and what happens when they enter a wrong code?

## 2 What you see first, and what second

First I see the large bold title "1. Sign up" at the top left. Second, my eye goes to the yellow diamond "Code correct?" because it has a thick pink border that makes it the loudest shape on the board. After that I read the row of boxes left to right and then the Key panel below.

## 3 The main path or structure from start to end, naming the shapes

The flow runs left to right in one row. It starts at "Landing page", a blue rounded box. An arrow goes to "Sign up with email or Google", a white rounded box. An arrow goes to "Verify the email code", another white rounded box. An arrow goes to "Code correct?", a yellow diamond with a pink outline. The Yes exit leads to "Email verified", a green rounded box, which is the end of the happy path.

## 4 Every decision or branch and where each exit goes

There is one decision, "Code correct?".

- Yes goes right to "Email verified", the green goal box.
- No drops down from the bottom of the diamond and runs right to "Wrong code error with resend", a white rounded box below "Email verified". From the left side of that error box a line runs left along the bottom and then up into the bottom of "Verify the email code", with an arrowhead there. So a wrong code sends the user back to the verify step to try again.

There are no other branches. Sign up by email and sign up by Google share one path.

## 5 What the key says and whether every mark is in the key

The Key panel lists five items:

- Blue rounded box: Where it starts.
- White rounded box: Screen or step.
- Yellow diamond: Decision.
- Green rounded box: Goal reached.
- Yellow diamond with a thick pink outline: In focus.

Every coloured shape on the board is covered by the key. The connectors are not in the key. They are thin grey solid lines with arrowheads and their meaning (flow from one step to the next) is easy to guess, but the key does not state it. The "Yes" and "No" labels on the lines are not in the key either, though they are self-explanatory. A footnote at the bottom gives the source (the Loomlist onboarding doc as described in the request) and says what was left out: expired codes, network failures and Google-specific steps.

## 6 Anything I could not read, could not follow or found ambiguous

- The pink "In focus" outline on the "Code correct?" diamond is explained in the key, but the board never says why this decision is in focus or what the reader should do about it.
- "Wrong code error with resend" packs two things into one box. It is unclear whether "resend" means the user must request a new code before returning to the verify step, or whether resend is an optional action on the error screen. The return line does not show a separate resend step.
- "Sign up with email or Google" leads straight into "Verify the email code". It is unclear whether a Google sign up also needs an email code. The footnote says Google-specific steps are left out, which partly explains this, but the board as drawn suggests Google users verify a code too.
- There is no limit or exit shown for repeated wrong codes, so the retry loop has no end other than success.
- The return line from the error box leaves from its left side and has its only arrowhead at "Verify the email code". The direction is readable but the line is long and runs under the main row, so it takes a second look to connect it to the error box.

All text on the board was readable at both sizes.

## 7 Could a product manager act on this board without asking anyone

Mostly yes, because the main path, the single decision and the wrong code retry loop are clear and keyed, but they would still need to ask how Google sign up differs and what "with resend" actually requires before building it.
