Before I draw anything, this is how I read the request.

Question: who does what, and where does the claim change hands?
Outcome: a CS or warehouse lead can see which team owns each step of a damaged-item refund and where the claim moves from CS to the warehouse.
Detail level, audience and distance: Balanced, Mixed and Laptop.
Settled by the sources: the support runbook gives the steps, the three lanes (customer, CS agent, warehouse operator) and one decision.
* The customer reports damage with a photo through the help form.
* The CS agent checks the photo. Clear damage is approved on the spot, and unclear damage sends the customer back to add a second photo.
* An approved claim is queued for the warehouse, where the operator books a courier, inspects the item on arrival and releases the refund.
* Every decision is logged in the claims table. I'll draw that table once, fed from the approval, and say in the source line that every decision writes to it.

Highlight: the CS step that approves the claim and hands it to the warehouse, which is the first glance. The refund release is the goal and stays unstyled.
Left out: pickup skipped under €20, a pilot that is not live. The runbook says nothing about a claim CS declines, so the board shows no decline path.
Assumed: CS and warehouse leads as the reader, and the three dial settings above. A different reader or a different place to read the board would settle either.
Still open: none that blocks drawing.
