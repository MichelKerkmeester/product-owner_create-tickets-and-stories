# v1.0.9.0, Read the Board Twice

Claude Sonnet 5.5 high reran on v1.0.8.0 the 14 scenarios that had failed and passed 7 under the operator's two grading rulings. The rest failed on the states inside a composite, a split title spelled two ways, a revision's second glance and title, replies that opened on prose above the URL, two stickies on adjacent rows and a missing-route gate no example showed. This release closes each one at its source, and the playbook now grades lanes, zones and a composite's states one way with two readers per board.

## What's New at a Glance

- **A composite's states read second.** With no state named, a state board plans its first state first, the states inside a composite second with their section and the end states last. The section itself stays part of the frame
- **One spelling for a split.** The second board of a split is `2. Workspace setup` everywhere, and the gate and the reply name it under `Left out:` even when both boards sit in the same file
- **A revision's change wins the second glance.** When the change is an end other than a failure end, that end alone is the second glance and the entry and the decisions move to the last glance. A revision that deletes the old section keeps the old title, so the delete question finds the old section
- **The reply opens on the board URL.** Nothing comes before it, not a heading, a summary or a note, and the reply after a delete opens on the board that stays and repeats the `Left out:` line
- **The label check says when it skipped.** `flow_check.py` prints `Label: skipped` with the reason when node is missing or the trace fails, so the model reads its labels by hand instead of trusting a silent check

## Changed

- A sticky already placed takes its side, so when two noted nodes sit on adjacent rows the second sticky goes below or to the left of its node and the reply names it on a `Finding:` line
- The gantt example shows the gate that names a missing route in place of the plan and the calls
- The style guide's 56 pixel rule holds on the composer's path estimate. FigJam routes each elbow itself and may draw two runs closer, which a grader does not count
- The rubric reads lanes and zones as frame on every type, so the glance lines plan the nodes inside them
- The playbook's Composition row reads lanes and zones as frame, counts a composite's states as parts and costs a point only when both of a board's two readers read a part early or out of order
