# v1.0.13.0, Drawn Glance Order and Whole Sketches

Claude Sonnet 5.5 high reran FJ-017, FJ-022, FJ-023 and FJ-036 on v1.0.12.0 and passed none. Three gates showed a sketch that dropped lines of the checked model, the glance plan put last the nodes the composer draws early, a flagged label had no branch when no shorter wording kept the source's words, a sticky past an end dot stood further than one sticky width from its node and an intake round ended on a sentence promising a round that never came. This release fixes each one.

## What's New at a Glance

- **The glance lines follow the drawn board.** On a user flow or a process the check prints a `Glance:` line, the order a reader meets the nodes on the board: the accent first, then column by column along the flow and down each column, with the goal marked. The second glance names the nodes up to and including the goal in that order, and the last glance names the rest, or the key and the source line when the goal comes last
- **A bent row is named.** A `Row:` line, never a failure, names a line between two main-row steps that bends off the row and the node standing between them. On a zoned flow it names the move into the next step's zone that straightens it
- **A label that may strand a word can be reworded.** A `Label:` line says `may leave` when only one of the two widths FigJam wraps at strands the word, and the label may be reworded in the source's words even where the new one runs longer. A label no such wording clears is kept and named at the gate
- **The gate shows the sketch whole.** The sketch is the checked model exactly as the check read it, every `%%` and `style` line included, with the `PASSED` line as it printed

## Changed

- `Sides:` lines name the alignment, top aligned or centred beside the node and left aligned or centred below or above it, and no sticky square stands further than one sticky width (240 px) from its node. The notes call places each sticky on the side and in the alignment its line named
- The three dials, the detail level, the audience and the reading distance, are asked in intake round one
- The user flow and process examples are relabelled so the check prints no `Label:` line: `Within 30 days of delivery?`, `Drop-off point or pickup?`, `Choose your drop-off point` and `Book a courier to pick it up` on the user flow and `Damage clear in the photo?`, `Send the second photo` and `Book a courier` on the process. Their `### Check` blocks show the real output with the `Glance:` line, and their glance lines follow the drawn order
- Every example gate's `Sketch:` line names the Section 4 model in full, every `%%` and `style` line included, with the check's `PASSED` line as it printed
- A new self-test checks that every worked example's `### Check` block equals what the check prints
- A line that skips a step on its way along the row, past a node that sits on the row or leads on to the line's end, is never named on a `Row:` line, since the composer bends it on purpose. An accented end that is also the goal is marked `(accent, goal)` on the `Glance:` line
- A top-down flow reads row by row and left to right, and on a revision that changes an end every node but the changed end goes to the last glance line, the entry and the decisions included
- A sticky past an end dot stays there only within one sticky width of its node, else it takes the next side, and a side the `Sides:` line named that is taken at the write passes to the next side in the same order, named on a `Finding:` line
- The playbook's Composition row plans the glance order from the `Glance:` line and reads parts a reader names in one reading step as a set. Its Legibility row scores 1 for one or more awkward wraps. A sketch that drops a `%%` or `style` line misses the gate signal, and text after the question of an intake round the scenario does not name misses the playback signal
