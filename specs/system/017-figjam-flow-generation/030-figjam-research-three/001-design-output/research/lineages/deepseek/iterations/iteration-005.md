Lineage: `deepseek`. Builds on: iteration 001 finding 11 (the scorecard's Layout is a crossing counter), iteration 002 finding 9 (a score with a reason is not a finding with a location), iteration 003 findings 7, 10, 11 and 12 (glance order, review scale, the two-zoom review and the geometry audit), and iteration 004 findings 2, 6, 8, 9, 13 (wrap-from-selection, board composition numbers, the Mermaid lever table, ELK's limits, and pre-write crossing judgment). ## Focus

How is board quality measured today across `references/quality-rubric.md` and the manual-testing-playbook scorecard, and what would measure it better?

## Actions Taken

1. Re-read `references/quality-rubric.md` and the playbook's Section 3 Board Quality Scorecard side by side, and mapped each scored dimension to the rule that produces it.
2. Read the pre-write checker's structure to find where a layout measure could live: `scripts/flow_check.py` (`check_block`, the per-profile dispatch, the direction-family gate) and `scripts/figjam_checks/common.py` (`MAX_LABEL_CHARS`, the contrast helpers).
3. Checked what the checker already computes — nodes, connectors, participants, messages, entities, tasks, sections, direction — because a measure that reuses those is much cheaper than one that needs a new parse.
4. Compared both against the two measurement methods the corpus supplies: the two-zoom review and the geometry audit (iteration 003), and the review-checklist report format (iteration 002).
5. Assembled the ranked change list from findings in all five iterations, each row naming the Product Owner file it changes and the source it comes from.

## Findings

1. **The scorecard measures the three things a machine can prove and one thing a stranger can answer, and everything else in between is unmeasured.** Fidelity is "every read-back count matches the model"; Reply is the shape of the reply line; Hygiene is "no generated node moved, resized or restyled"; Clarity is "a fresh reader… answers the scenario's three questions". Between Fidelity and Clarity sits the entire question of whether the board has a beginning, a grouping and a breathing rhythm, and no dimension asks it. [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:79] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:83]

2. **Clarity is the closest thing to a reading test, and its three questions are content questions, not hierarchy questions.** Every scenario's questions are of the form "What does a customer see more than 30 days after delivery?", "Which two ways can the item go back?" — answerable by scanning the labels in any order. A reader can score 3 of 3 on a board whose first glance is a footnote, which is precisely the failure the corpus's two-zoom review is built to catch. [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/board-quality/user-flow-board-quality.md:31] [SOURCE: research/corpus/prasantloki__figjam-builder/references/composition-grammar.md:106]

3. **Layout is a single counter with three bands, and it conflates three different failures.** "2: no overlap, no connector crossing and one reading direction that matches the model. 1: no overlap and one crossing. 0: any overlap, or two or more crossings" treats a crossing, a shared trunk that makes edge ownership ambiguous, and a connector label sitting on its stroke as the same defect — or as no defect when the count is low. The corpus explicitly separates them: "Avoid shared trunks when they make edge ownership ambiguous", "Keep labels at the 24px metadata floor, offset from paths and nodes", and "Judge connector crossings from the rendered screenshot; a connector bounding box does not describe its path". [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:81] [SOURCE: research/corpus/prasantloki__figjam-builder/references/composition-grammar.md:98]

4. **An unexamined dimension scores a silent 2, and a scored dimension with no location cannot be repaired.** The rubric's own honesty standard is higher than the scorecard's: it says "A missing tier reads as 'not checked'", and every finding must carry "a location, the offending code, the fix, and the criterion". The scorecard reports seven numbers and one reason per score. A "Layout 1" tells a later operator that something crossed, not which connector or which pair of nodes, so the next session cannot fix it without re-deriving the whole review. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-fundamentals/references/review-checklist.md:149] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-fundamentals/references/review-checklist.md:17] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:72]

5. **Hygiene is a compound dimension, so one broken clause hides the other three.** "2: no generated node moved, resized or restyled between the first and the last read, no extension covers a label, nothing outside the write changed and nothing was written to `export/`. 0: any of these broken." Four independent invariants share a single bit, which means a board can lose the dimension for the least important of them while the report cannot say which. The packet's own `fileProtection` block already enumerates these as separate properties, so the atomic version is available for free. [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:84] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/flow-mode.md:246]

6. **Nothing is measured at two zooms, so a board is only ever read the way its author already reads it.** Every scenario screenshots "at 100% zoom". The corpus's review method requires an overview pass specifically because "If that order differs from the plan, revise scale, position, contrast, or grouping" — a judgment that cannot be made at reading zoom, where the author is standing too close to see that the first glance landed on a legend instead of the decision. [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:80] [SOURCE: research/corpus/prasantloki__figjam-builder/references/composition-grammar.md:106]

7. **There is no scored board to compare against, so scores are not comparable across sessions or types.** The packet ships text models (`assets/examples/`, `assets/templates/`) that are checked by `flow_check.py`, and board-quality scenarios produce a score per run — but no board is kept as a scored reference. The corpus's typography method needs an anchor for exactly this reason: rendered-size targets only mean something against a normalised overview of a known board. Without an anchor, a 12-of-14 has no meaning beyond "someone thought it was fine". [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/SKILL.md:198] [SOURCE: research/corpus/prasantloki__figjam-builder/references/typography-grammar.md:24]

8. **Several board qualities are computable from what the checker already parses, so better measurement does not require a new machine.** `check_block` already returns a profile, a size string and a direction, counting nodes, connectors, participants, messages, entities, tasks and sections, and it already enforces one direction family across diagrams of one flow. From that same parse a second group of measures is free: rank width (the widest set of nodes at one depth), fan-out per node, edges-per-node ratio, longest path, the count of label characters against the 40-character cap already held in `MAX_LABEL_CHARS`, and a must-cross edge-pair estimate from the edge set. Those are exactly the numbers a floating-point scorecard cannot produce and a pre-write slot can. [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/scripts/flow_check.py:100] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/scripts/figjam_checks/common.py:21]

9. **A measured score should ship with the fixture that produced it, and the packet already has the place for it.** The design comparator keeps its scored corpus in `benchmark/reports/<date>--<playbook-id>/` with `results.csv` and a findings file per run, so a regression is a diff rather than an opinion. `sk-create-figjam`'s playbook records a scorecard verdict per session but keeps no series, so nobody can say whether the board quality is rising. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/benchmark/reports/README.md] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:87]

10. **The scorecard's pass condition already has the right shape: it is a floor plus three dimensions that must be perfect.** "A scored board passes at 12 of 14 or more, with Fidelity, Hygiene and Reply at 2 and no dimension at 0." That structure — a total floor, plus mandatory-perfect dimensions, plus a per-dimension floor of zero — is a good design and should survive any change to what the dimensions are. What it is missing is a mandatory dimension that covers composition, which is the one thing a reader notices before any count. [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:87]

## Questions Answered

- "How is board quality measured today across references/quality-rubric.md and the manual-testing-playbook scorecard, and what would measure it better?" — answered. Today: seven 0/1/2 dimensions, one screenshot at 100% zoom, one reason per score, a total floor of 12 with three dimensions held at 2. It measures fidelity, reply shape and hygiene well, and composition, grouping, whitespace, reading order and title presence not at all. Better: a planned-versus-actual glance order at two zoom levels, a Composition dimension, atomic hygiene checks with a location and a fix per lost point, and computed density and crossing-potential numbers from the parse the checker already performs — recorded per run so quality becomes a series rather than an opinion.

## Questions Remaining

None of the five key questions is unanswered. The synthesis carries one open item deliberately: whether a diagram can be generated inside a locked section is unobserved, so it is reported as a route to verify rather than a recommended change (iteration 004, finding 5).

## Ruled Out This Iteration

- **Adding a floating-point quality score.** A decimal built from seven judgements reads as precision the evidence cannot support; the existing floor-plus-mandatory-dimensions shape is more honest and should be kept.
- **Making the checker score the board.** The board is an image and a node list; the checker sees Mermaid text. Computed measures belong pre-write, and reader measures belong in the playbook.
- **Scoring every dimension with the same effort.** Fidelity and Reply are cheap and mechanical; the glance-order test needs a fresh reader. The scorecard should say which dimensions are machine-checked and which need a person.
