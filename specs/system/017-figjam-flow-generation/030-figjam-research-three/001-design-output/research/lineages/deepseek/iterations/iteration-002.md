# Iteration 002 — Which design rules have no FigJam counterpart, and which of them a board can carry

Lineage: `deepseek`. Builds on: iteration 001, findings 3 (spacing unowned), 5 (grouping type-gated), 6 (density is a count) and 11 (scorecard has no reading-quality dimension). This iteration goes to the comparators named in the dispatch and asks, rule by rule, whether a FigJam board can carry the rule at all — so later iterations only rank changes that a board can actually hold.

## Focus

Which diagram design rules in `sk-design-diagram` and `sk-design-fundamentals` have no counterpart in the FigJam packet, and which of those gaps can a FigJam board actually carry?

## Findings

1. **The comparator states a minimum gap between nodes as a number; the FigJam packet states none.** `output-spec.md` fixes "Min gap between nodes" at 24 for the standard ramp, 40 for a presentation ramp and 24 for print, alongside a 40px outer margin and a 60px legend strip. The FigJam packet's only gaps are extension clearances (iteration 001, finding 3). The generator owns node geometry, so the *gap itself* is not steerable — but the reason a gap collapses into the 40px band is that the model put too many nodes on one rank or too long a label on one box, and both of those are steerable. The carryable form of the rule is a model-side constraint that protects the gap, not a gap measurement. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/references/foundations/output-spec.md:79] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/references/foundations/output-spec.md:65] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/hybrid-workflow.md:114]

2. **Proximity is the comparator's named fix for ambiguous grouping, and the FigJam packet has no proximity rule at all.** The diagnosis table resolves "Ambiguous grouping — which label goes with which field, which heading owns this paragraph" with one sentence: "More space *around* a group than *within* it". The FigJam packet does own a spacing decision that could express proximity — a status tint is drawn "24 pixels larger on every side" — but it never states the relationship the tint should have to its contents' own spacing, so a tint can hug two nodes that are 200 pixels apart and read as one loose rectangle rather than a group. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-fundamentals/references/diagnosis-table.md:50] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/hybrid-workflow.md:118]

3. **The comparator zones a diagram above 9 nodes; the FigJam packet only ever splits.** `output-spec.md` makes zoning mandatory above 9 nodes — "every node belongs to a labeled zone (2–4 zones, hairline-bordered, `paper-2` fill, mono uppercase zone label at top-left)" — and says why: "An unzoned 20-node diagram is a wiring diagram, not a schematic." The FigJam packet's answer to the same pressure is always a split: "Over budget, split rather than shrink", and for a user flow "A longer flow splits into diagrams that hand off through a named end and a start with the same label". Splitting is a stronger default than zoning, but it produces a board of small diagrams with no visible relationship between them (iteration 001, finding 7), which is exactly what zoning was invented to prevent. The gap is that no FigJam type has a zoning option between "one diagram" and "two diagrams". [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/references/foundations/output-spec.md:103] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/flow-mode.md:170] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/types/user-flow.md:77]

4. **The comparator has five mandatory connector rules; the FigJam packet has one proxy for all five.** The mandatory set is: orthogonal right-angle connectors only, a 6–10px visible gap between an arrow label and its stroke with an opaque mask behind the label, no two connectors sharing a stroke path, fanned attach points at least 12px apart on a shared edge, and no connector routed behind a non-endpoint box. The FigJam packet's parallel is a single scorecard line — "no connector crossing" for a 2, "one crossing" for a 1 — plus a read-back that checks endpoints and end caps. Because the generator routes, four of the five are not steerable; what *is* steerable is whether the model creates the conditions where they fail: a long label on a connector inside a dense rank, two edges between the same pair of nodes, or a declaration order that forces an edge to reach across the diagram. The packet currently names none of those as model-side causes. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:291] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:292] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:294] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:295] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:81]

5. **The comparator carries a density dial with a target, not just a ceiling.** `sk-design-diagram` states "density at target 4/10 — enough to be technically complete, not so dense it needs a guide. Above 9 nodes it's probably two diagrams", where 4/10 is a stated target a reviewer can judge against rather than a cap. The FigJam packet has ceilings (9 nodes, 12 gantt tasks, 5 parallel bars) and no target, so the only two states it can express are "inside budget" and "split". A board drawn at 2/10 and a board drawn at 9/10 pass the same check. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:264] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/types/user-flow.md:64]

6. **Shape-as-hierarchy is a hard comparator rule that four of the seven FigJam types cannot satisfy, and the packet never says so.** The comparator: "NEVER draw identical boxes for every node — node type → treatment encodes hierarchy." A user flow has four shapes with fixed meanings and a process adds lanes and stores, so those two types are genuinely stronger than the comparator here. But a sequence diagram has lifelines and arrows, an ER diagram has one table shape, a state diagram has rounded states and one start/end marker, and a gantt chart has bars. In those four types, every node of the same kind draws identically, so all hierarchy has to arrive from the extension layer — and the packet treats extensions as optional. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:282] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/types/user-flow.md:40] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/types/process.md:58]

7. **The comparator requires a legend that covers every type used and nothing extra; the FigJam legend is a column of numbered stickies and covers only annotations.** The comparator's rule is "Legend covers every type used — and nothing extra", and the legend is a horizontal strip inside the `viewBox` with the height expanded by about 60px. The FigJam packet's legend is scoped to pins ("a small numbered circle sits on each annotated node's top-left corner, and the matching stickies stand in a column to the right"), and it explicitly keeps the strip out ("The bottom legend strip" is listed under what did not translate). So a board that uses four shapes, a lane and a status tint has no legend for any of them, and the reader is expected to infer the vocabulary. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:358] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:189] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/hybrid-workflow.md:66] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/style-guide.md:130]

8. **The comparator gives every diagram a title and a one-sentence description as a delivery contract; the FigJam packet gives the board neither.** `sk-design-diagram`'s accessible SVG contract requires a `<title>` that is "the short name of the subject — roughly the page `<h1>`, ~60 characters or fewer" and a `<desc>` that is "one sentence stating what the diagram shows in reader terms — describe content, not geometry". The FigJam equivalents exist but are unused as a contract: the generator's `name` argument, the `userIntent` sentence that is already sent on every call, and the optional title text node. Nothing requires the title node and `name` to be the same string, and nothing puts the `userIntent` sentence on the board or in the reply. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:246] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/SKILL.md:247] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/flow-mode.md:186] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/hybrid-workflow.md:150]

9. **The comparator's review contract demands a location, an offending value and a fix per finding; the scorecard produces a number per dimension.** `review-checklist.md` opens with "Every finding names a file, a line, the criterion it violates, and the fix" and its report format requires "a count of zero explicitly rather than omitting the tier. A missing tier reads as 'not checked'". The playbook scorecard gives seven 0/1/2 dimensions with "one reason per score" [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:72] — so "Layout 1" is a score with a reason, not a finding with a location. A board that lost a point cannot be repaired from the score alone, and a dimension that was never examined scores a silent 2. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-fundamentals/references/review-checklist.md:17] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-fundamentals/references/review-checklist.md:149] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/manual-testing-playbook/manual-testing-playbook.md:79]

10. **The comparator scales its type ramp with the reading distance; the FigJam packet has no size dial, and this is the one place its own "two dials" claim understates the problem.** `output-spec.md` scales node names from 12 to 16 to 12 across standard, presentation and print ramps, and draws the consequence: "Presentation ramp implies fewer nodes — 16px names in 64px boxes eat the canvas." The FigJam packet says four dials reduce to two because "A FigJam board fixes the format and the size". That is right about format and optimistic about size: a board read on a projector, or a board holding six diagrams, is further away than a single diagram read on a laptop, and its node names do not grow. Since the generator's font cannot be changed afterwards (iteration 001, finding 9), the *only* way to honour the comparator's insight is to reduce node count and label length when the board is meant for a wall — a decision the packet's current dials cannot express. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/references/foundations/output-spec.md:71] [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-diagram/references/foundations/output-spec.md:81] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/flow-mode.md:148]

11. **The comparator's status-tint treatment is the inverse of the FigJam one, and the FigJam direction is measurably worse.** `color-system.md` says the default for status pills and badges is "dark colored text on a light colored tint", because "A green `800` on a green `100` easily clears AAA, keeps the semantic color, and sits quietly in the hierarchy". The FigJam palette does the same thing for its accent pairs — light fill, charcoal text — and measures 11.21:1 to 14.61:1, which is a strength. [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/style-guide.md:64] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/style-guide.md:66] But the *status tint* recipe paints a rounded rectangle behind a group in the accent pair's own colours, so a tint covers area at the same saturation as the accent that marks the focal node. The style guide's own departure table already admits seven strokes sit under the 3.0:1 mark gate; a full-field tint multiplies that area by the size of the group. [SOURCE: .skilled/skills/sk-design/sk-design-fundamentals/references/color-system.md:126] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/style-guide.md:63] [SOURCE: AI Systems/Product Owner/sk-create-figjam/references/hybrid-workflow.md:118]

12. **The comparator's strongest single instruction is one the FigJam packet must not adopt, and saying so is itself the finding.** "NEVER reproduce a Mermaid renderer layout — imports get an editorial layout; automatic spacing and routing are replaced, not copied." The FigJam packet's whole architecture is the opposite: hand the model to the generator and let it lay the diagram out. Adopting the comparator rule would mean abandoning `generate_diagram`, which the packet's own type files make impossible for architecture (the grid layout is the only way it draws). The transferable part is narrower and more useful: where the comparator rejects a renderer's layout, it is rejecting *automatic spacing that ignores grouping*. A board can honour that intent without replacing the renderer, by giving the generator a model that has fewer competing ranks and a grouping the reader can see. ## Questions Answered

- "Which diagram design rules in sk-design-diagram and sk-design-fundamentals have no counterpart in the FigJam packet, and which of those gaps can a FigJam board actually carry?" — answered. Twelve rules were checked. Four transfer whole (proximity as the grouping fix, the title-and-description contract, the legend-covers-the-vocabulary rule, the per-finding review format). Five transfer only as a model-side constraint, because the generator owns the geometry they measure (minimum gap, the five connector rules, the type ramp, zoning, density target). Two transfer inverted (the status tint, and shape-as-hierarchy, which four types cannot satisfy). One must be refused (replacing the renderer's layout).

## Ruled Out This Iteration

- **Adopting "replace the renderer's layout" as a FigJam rule.** The generator is the only route for architecture, and the packet's type files depend on it. Direction for later iterations: constrain the model so the generator's default is good, rather than take layout back.
- **Re-adding the horizontal legend strip and the `viewBox` expansion.** A board has no `viewBox`; the board-native form is a legend group the reader can see without scrolling, which iteration 4 will size.
- **Re-adding the four-dial output spec verbatim.** Format and size genuinely do not transfer as written; only the reading-distance consequence of the size dial does (finding 10).
