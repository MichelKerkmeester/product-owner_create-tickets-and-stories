# Rules - Conciseness - v0.100

The conciseness layer above the Human Voice card: what to cut, what is never cut, and the test that decides which is which.

**Loading Condition:** ALWAYS, and rules only. A file paid for on every turn stays small enough to be worth paying for
**Purpose:** Carries the cut rules, the keep rules and the reconstruction test that decides between them, so every delivery is sized as well as checked for banned wording
**Scope:** Quantity, structure and load-bearing-ness, so an artifact that clears every hard blocker is not still three times too long. Nothing routes to it, since being ALWAYS-loaded is what makes it unroutable
**Output Path:** None. The layer governs the length of an artifact another file writes, and produces none of its own
**Loads With:** the consuming system's skill entry and `Rules - Human Voice Core`. That card governs word choice and this file governs quantity, so both run on every delivery
**Authority:** `Rules - Conciseness - On Demand Rationale - v0.100` carries the reason behind every rule below, the vocabulary candidates that were refused, the worked cut and keep pairs, and the roster of what blocks against what advises. Load it ON_DEMAND before changing a rule, never in order to write one. Both files share one numbering, so a section present in one and absent here lives there and keeps its number. That is why this card runs 5, 7, 8, 12 rather than counting straight, and why renumbering it would break the Section 8 citations three skills carry

---

## 1. THE TWO-SIDED CONTRACT

Cuts and keeps here are edits rather than scored penalties, so they never count toward a system's hard blocker total. The two halves are load-bearing in equal measure: read only the cut half and the output arrives compressed until the reasoning falls out of it, and read only the keep half and nothing ever gets shorter. Every rule the validator blocks on arrives in a pair at the same strength, so the gate cannot be satisfied by cutting alone.

---

## 2. THE RECONSTRUCTION TEST

After a cut, could a reader who does not already know the answer recover what was removed from what remains? If yes, the cut was free. If no, the information moved into the reader's head, which is loss wearing the shape of compression. Apply it per cut and never per document, because a document-level verdict is a judgement about length and length is the judgement that strips connectives. The reader it asks about is the one who opens the artifact three weeks later with no memory of the conversation that produced it.

---

## 3. CUT RULES

### 3.1 The named rules

- **Question echo.** An opening that restates the prompt before answering it
- **Intent narration.** Announcing the move instead of making it
- **Compliance acknowledgement.** Agreeing to do the work at the top of the work
- **Terminal recap.** A closing paragraph that repeats the body, even accurately
- **Hedge stack.** Two or more uncertainty markers on one claim. Keep the one that is true
- **Importance assertion.** A sentence whose only content is that something matters
- **The conclusion move.** The whole paragraph, not only the phrase that opens it
- **Both-sides padding.** Symmetric pro and con on a question that is not symmetric
- **Empty enumeration.** Naming that there are several things, listing them, analysing none
- **Heading echo.** A first sentence that restates its own heading
- **Restated constraint.** Repeating back a fact the user supplied
- **Effort reporting.** Narrating the work done rather than what it found
- **Count padding.** An item added so a list carries a different number of items. The triad rule counts triads per 150 words and bans none, so leave the count alone and cut something that was never load-bearing

### 3.2 The stated vocabulary

The validator blocks on exactly the terms below, so this list and the gate are one list, and adding a term to either alone is the drift that kept these checks advisory. Section 3.3 of the on-demand rationale records every candidate refused, with the correct writing each would have failed.

Never open a sentence with: "In conclusion", "In summary", "To summarize", "To summarise", "To recap", "To sum up", "It's worth noting", "It is worth noting", "It's important to note", "It is important to note", "Let's explore", "Let's dive in", "Let's take a look", "At the end of the day", "Without further ado", "As we all know", "It goes without saying", "First and foremost", "Last but not least", "With that in mind", "On that note", "That said", "Needless to say", "Simply put", "In essence", "At its core", "In the world of", "When it comes to", "At a high level", "As you can see", "As mentioned above", "As noted above", "As previously noted", "As we have seen", "As discussed above", "In this section", "Put simply", "Generally speaking", "Broadly speaking", "The key takeaway", "The fact of the matter is", "It should be noted", "It must be noted", "By way of background".

Never close a section on: "In summary", "In conclusion", "To summarize", "To summarise", "To recap", "To sum up", "In short", "All in all", "Overall", "Ultimately", "In closing", "To conclude", "To wrap up", "The takeaway", "Net net", "In a nutshell", "Long story short", "To put it simply", "On the whole".

At most one hedge per claim: "might", "may", "could", "perhaps", "maybe", "possibly", "potentially", "probably", "seems", "appears", "arguably", "somewhat", "relatively", "presumably", "likely", "generally", "typically", "I think", "I believe", "tends to", "to some extent", "in some cases", "apparently", "conceivably", "ostensibly", "seemingly", "in theory", "I suspect", "My sense is".

A cross-reference that names its target stays legitimate, so "As noted above" is banned while "the payout window defined under The weekly cycle" is not. A heading followed by a sentence that shares its words and adds no new one is a heading echo: cut the sentence and open on substance.

### 3.4 The two cut rules the validator settles alone

Both resolve without judgement, so the validator blocks on them in a deliverable and reports them as advice on an instruction file.

**Importance assertion.** A sentence of twelve words or fewer carrying one of "important", "critical", "essential", "crucial", "vital", "matters", "significant", "noteworthy" or "fundamental", and carrying no content word of its own beyond that marker and generic filler. A sentence that names what matters and why is left alone.

**Effort reporting.** A sentence opening on "I" or "We" that reaches one of "analysed", "analyzed", "reviewed", "checked", "scored", "scanned", "verified", "examined", "evaluated", "assessed", "investigated" or "audited" within the next few words. A quoted mention is left alone, and so is a sentence whose subject is the process the artifact documents rather than the author. State what the work found, since the work itself is not the deliverable.

## 4. KEEP RULES

The anti-robotic half, at the same weight as the cut list. Each item names what a compression pass destroys first.

- **Semantic connectives.** `because`, `so`, `which means`, `unless`, `otherwise`, `since` carry the logic, and deleting one turns an argument into a pile of assertions. The Human Voice cap on `however`, `furthermore`, `moreover`, `additionally` and `consequently` limits rhetoric and never reaches these
- **One concrete instance per non-obvious rule.** The example is what makes a rule applicable
- **The caveat that prevents a wrong action.** A reader who follows the trimmed version and breaks something was not served by the trim
- **The scope qualifier.** `when`, `only when`, `for X but not Y`. A rule stripped of its scope is applied everywhere
- **The noun behind a pronoun.** Name it again once the referent sits more than a clause away
- **The negative case.** "A path string is not verification" is where the reader learns the boundary
- **The specific number.** 33 fixtures, not several fixtures
- **One human beat per roughly 400 words.** An aside, a judgement, a plain admission of what is unknown
- **Grammar.** Articles, copulas and finite verbs are never a compression target, because conciseness removes claims and never syntax

## 5. OVER-COMPRESSION SIGNATURES

### 5.1 The five shapes

- **Connective stripping.** Clauses sit next to each other with the relation deleted, so the reader guesses whether the second causes, qualifies or contradicts the first
- **Referent stripping.** Pronouns and bare nouns left with their antecedents cut away
- **Telegraphese.** Dropped articles and copulas, verbless fragments, headline grammar in body text
- **Condition collapse.** A conditional rule flattened into an absolute one, which is a correctness bug rather than a style one
- **Example deletion.** An abstract rule left with nothing showing what it looks like in use

### 5.2 The two the validator counts, and the perimeter they are measured over

Connective stripping and telegraphese leave a numeric trace, so the validator counts them and blocks on a deliverable below either floor. Repair a document that trips one by naming the deleted relations back in and restoring the subject behind every stranded pronoun, never by adding words.

- **Connective floor.** At least 5 semantic connectives per 1000 words of prose
- **Article floor.** At least 30 articles per 1000 words of prose

Both floors are measured over paragraph prose alone, because a ratio only means something where sentences carry the reasoning. Outside the perimeter: frontmatter, a heading at any level, a bullet, a numbered item, a table row, a blockquote, an image, a horizontal rule, a field line such as `**Placement:** Creator deals dashboard`, a hashtag or handle run, a label line whose whole content is one bold or underlined run such as `**Most authentic**`, anything inside a fenced code block, and any HTML comment. Below 200 words of paragraph prose neither floor is applied, since a ratio over fewer words than that is noise.

---

## 7. FORMAT

Prose, bullets and tables are not interchangeable, and picking wrong is how a short answer still reads badly. Three questions decide and the first yes wins: prose where the items differ in weight or their order encodes reasoning, a table where every row shares the same fields, bullets where the items are parallel, independently actionable and three to seven in number. Anything reaching the end without a yes is one paragraph pretending to be a list. Over-bulleting is the main way output turns robotic while looking concise, and the tells are verbless fragments, a bullet that needs the one above it to parse, and a list whose connectives were deleted so the items would line up. Heading density scales with length: under 300 words no headings, above that one per 150 to 400 words.

## 8. WHAT A DELIVERABLE NEVER CONTAINS

The reasoning that produced an artifact, the score it earned and the assumptions it rests on are real work, and they belong in the reply beside the export path rather than inside the file the reader opens.

### 8.1 The enumeration

- A scoring section of any kind, whether headed MEQT, HVR, DEAL scoring, Scoring, Self-scan, Hard blockers or Weakest
- A dimension breakdown line, such as `M 4 · E 6 · Q 6 · T 3 · D 3` or its `D · E · A · L` equivalent
- A total, a floor status, a pass or fail verdict, or a score band action
- A hard blocker count or a voice self-scan line, in any wording
- Score commentary, such as a line opening `Weakest:` and naming which dimensions were thin
- An improvement-cycle note, an iteration count or a re-score history
- A bracketed `[Assumes: ...]` tag
- A methodology transcript, a perspective roster, an energy level or a phase-flow trace
- A validation checklist, a gate roster or a template-compliance report
- An attestation footer, a Mode header in visible text, a Template version in visible text, or any other processing metadata

### 8.2 The one sanctioned exception

Line 1 may carry a single HTML comment header, which renders as nothing and is the designed home for the mode, the framework, the score and the template version. A `Stats:` or `Assumes:` line written as an HTML comment directly below sits inside the same exception, for the same reason. Invisible when rendered is the whole test, so a score written as visible text is a violation wherever it sits in the file and a score written as a comment is fine wherever it sits.

### 8.3 Where the reporting goes instead

The reply carries what the artifact does not: the export path with read-back confirmation where the runtime can write files, the score and its verdict at the depth the mode calls for, the voice self-scan line naming the terms fixed and the terms kept with their reason, the assumptions in a form the user can correct before they harden, a short summary and one next step. Producing that report is the point, and moving it out of the artifact does not make it optional.

### 8.4 Two narrow overrides

Where the artifact refines a supplied source, never inject a Mode, Template, Perspectives, Quality Score or Energy header into preserved source structure, since the comment header is for a new artifact and a refinement keeps the source's own opening. Where the packaging renders the deliverable in a panel instead of saving a file, that rendered block is the artifact and the enumeration applies to it unchanged, the report follows the block, and the path is reported as export-equivalent rather than claimed as a save.

## 12. THE PRE-EXPORT PASS

Run this after the voice pass and before the export, in order. The first two steps are the cut side and the last three are the keep side, and running only the cut side is the failure this layer exists to prevent.

1. Delete every opener, recap and hedge stack named in Section 3.2, both shapes named in Section 3.4, and the question echo, intent narration, compliance acknowledgement and closing recap the body already carries, including the sentences after the phrase that opened one
2. Apply the reconstruction test to every remaining cut you are tempted to make, and stop at the first one that fails
3. Name every relation a reader would otherwise guess, then repair referent stripping, telegraphese and condition collapse in place
4. Confirm one concrete instance survives per non-obvious rule, one caveat survives per action that could go wrong, and the format matches the content under Section 7
5. Confirm the body carries nothing from Section 8.1, and that everything removed at this step now sits in the reply. This step is skipped most often, because the process material was correct work and deleting correct work feels wrong. It is not deleted, it moves
